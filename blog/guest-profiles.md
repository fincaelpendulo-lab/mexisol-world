## At a glance

- 399 registered past guests, 89% came for ayahuasca, median age 44 today, 52% men / 48% women.
- Ages: 20s 8%, 30s 26%, 40s 30%, 50s 23%, 60s 9%, 70+ 4%.
- Notes mention: depression/anxiety/trauma 39%, came with or via someone 31%, life transition 19%,
  spiritual practice/past retreats 15%, alcohol or addiction 13%, psilocybin retreat 10%. (Keyword estimates.)

## How they found us

- Friends, family and past guests (strongest by far). Google search and reviews (chose us over Peru or Costa Rica).
  Instagram/Facebook. Talks and events (PSYCON, a conference talk in Phoenix). Books (e.g. *Trippy*) and documentaries.
  Therapists and doctors.
- Why they picked us: Mexico over Peru (shorter flights, easier visas, lower total cost), price, reviews,
  the intake call, variety (ayahuasca plus Satori, Bufo, kambo, temazcal in one week).

## Profile 1: The Healing Seeker (about 39%, largest)

Ages 30 to 55, men and women equally, mostly US. Years of depression, anxiety, PTSD or childhood trauma. Tried therapy
and often antidepressants with partial results. About 1 in 8 were on an SSRI and needed a taper plan. Some veterans,
some treatment-resistant depression. Hopeful but cautious; want to know it is safe.
- Want: relief therapy and medication have not given; joy and meaning again; process trauma in a held space.
- Stops them: fear of the medicine and losing control; SSRI and tapering questions; safety and who runs ceremony.
- Find them: Google questions on ayahuasca, depression and medication; r/Ayahuasca, r/Psychonaut, r/TRD, r/CPTSD;
  mental health podcasts and YouTube testimonials; veteran groups and psychedelic therapy nonprofits.
- Reach them: long honest guides (SSRIs and ayahuasca, how screening works, what a hard night looks like); show
  the medical screening and intake call up front; free "Is this right for me?" call as the main CTA.
- Keywords: ayahuasca for depression; ayahuasca and SSRIs; how to taper off antidepressants before ayahuasca;
  plant medicine retreat for trauma; ayahuasca for PTSD; psilocybin retreat for depression; treatment resistant
  depression alternative; safe ayahuasca retreat; ayahuasca retreat with medical screening.
- WATCH: no cure claims. Talk about experiences and support, not treatment.

## Profile 2: The Crossroads (about 19%)

Ages 35 to 65, slightly more women. Divorce, breakup, a parent's death, job or business ended, or stuck and asking
what life is for. Often fine on paper. Want a reset and a clear next step.
- Want: clarity on what comes next; to grieve and let go; a fresh start (birthday, new year).
- Stops them: time off work; "is it the right moment?"; family who doesn't understand.
- Find them: Instagram/Pinterest (reset, healing journey, solo travel); divorce and grief communities, life coaches;
  podcasts on purpose and reinvention; retreat directories.
- Reach them: seasonal campaigns (January reset, birthday retreat, after the holidays); stories about one turning
  point, not about the medicine; dates far enough ahead to plan time off.
- Keywords: healing retreat after divorce; spiritual retreat for grief; retreat to find my purpose; life reset retreat
  Mexico; burnout retreat; solo healing retreat for women; what to do after a breakup retreat; new year retreat Mexico.

## Profile 3: The Pattern Breaker (about 13%)

Ages 30 to 60, mostly men. Alcohol, cocaine or other habits, or sober but still sees addictive patterns. Some come as
an alternative to rehab. Often a family member suggests or pays. Needs the most careful screening.
- Want: break a cycle for good; understand the pain behind the habit; make their family proud.
- Stops them: shame; fear they can't stop before arrival; low motivation when pushed by someone else.
- Find them: family members searching on their behalf; addiction counselors and sober coaches; recovery podcasts and
  r/stopdrinking; men's groups and veteran networks.
- Reach them: content for families ("how to support someone considering a retreat"); clear rules (arrive sober; we are
  not a detox center); integration after the retreat.
- Keywords: ayahuasca for addiction; plant medicine for alcoholism; ayahuasca vs ibogaine; retreat to quit drinking;
  alternative to rehab Mexico; how to help a family member with addiction.
- WATCH: only for people who can arrive alcohol free. Never position the retreat as rehab or detox.

## Profile 4: The Returning Journeyer (about 15%)

Ages 30 to 60, men and women. Meditates, yoga, kundalini, reiki. Has sat with ayahuasca in Peru, Costa Rica or
elsewhere, or deep psilocybin experience. Some do a retreat every year or two. Well informed, compares centers.
- Want: depth and authentic ceremony; new medicines (Satori, Bufo, kambo); a calm group without drama.
- Stops them: loyalty to another center; doubts about facilitators' lineage; group size and dynamics.
- Find them: Retreat Guru, BookRetreats, AyaAdvisors; PSYCON, Psychedelic Science; yoga studios; past guests.
- Reach them: comparison pages (Mexico vs Peru, what makes our week different); lineage, ceremony structure, Satori.
- Keywords: best ayahuasca retreat Mexico; ayahuasca retreat near the US; ayahuasca Mexico vs Peru; affordable
  ayahuasca retreat; Rythmia alternative; Satori DMT retreat; Bufo ceremony Mexico; kambo and ayahuasca retreat.

## Profile 5: The Caregiver (about 15%)

Ages 30 to 60, mostly women. Nurses (ICU, ER, end of life), PAs, therapists, psychologists, addiction counselors,
trauma workers. Burnout and secondary trauma. Some want to become facilitators. Ask precise medical questions.
- Want: to be cared for instead of caring; recovery from burnout; professional insight into psychedelic work.
- Stops them: licensing and career worries; clinical skepticism about safety; hard to get a week off.
- Find them: LinkedIn/Facebook groups for nurses and therapists; psychedelic therapy trainings; ketamine clinics, IFS.
- Reach them: "care for the caregiver" messaging; publish the medical screening protocol in detail.
- Keywords: retreat for nurses burnout; healing retreat for therapists; psychedelic retreat for healthcare workers;
  compassion fatigue retreat; ayahuasca experience for therapists; how to become a plant medicine facilitator.

## Profile 6: The Psilocybin-Curious Woman (about 10%, fastest to grow)

Ages 30 to 60, mostly women. Drawn to mushrooms over ayahuasca: less purging, gentler reputation, often microdosing
or home experience. Many in perimenopause or menopause or living with PMDD or endometriosis. Several want a
women-only space and same-gender support.
- Want: a safe, women-led space; reconnection with body and intuition; a deeper dose than at home.
- Stops them: fear of ayahuasca's purging; safety as a solo female traveler; legality questions.
- Find them: Instagram/TikTok (microdosing, menopause, women's circles); women's wellness podcasts; yoga and
  breathwork studios, doulas; perimenopause and PMDD communities.
- Reach them: psilocybin vs ayahuasca for first-timers; safety, transport and same-gender support.
- Keywords: women's psilocybin retreat; psilocybin retreat Mexico; legal mushroom retreat; psilocybin retreat for
  menopause; microdosing to macrodose retreat; psilocybin vs ayahuasca; safe retreat for solo women travelers.

## Profile 7: The Local Seeker (at least 5%, likely more)

Ages 25 to 55. Guadalajara, Monterrey, CDMX, Spanish speaking, plus expats in Mexico (Ajijic, San Pancho, CDMX).
Can drive or take a short flight, value being with other Mexicans, often can't afford US pricing.
- Want: ceremony close to home; Spanish-speaking facilitators; fair pricing in pesos.
- Stops them: a week off work; price in dollars; family and religious stigma.
- Find them: Instagram/Facebook in Spanish; WhatsApp groups, temazcal and yoga circles in Jalisco; expat groups in
  Ajijic and Puerto Vallarta; Finca El Péndulo events.
- Reach them: Spanish pages, MXN prices, WhatsApp booking; weekend formats; events at the finca.
- Keywords (Spanish): retiro de ayahuasca en Jalisco; ceremonia de ayahuasca Guadalajara; retiro de hongos México;
  retiro de psilocibina; temazcal cerca de Guadalajara; retiro espiritual fin de semana; ayahuasca Ajijic.

## Strategy notes that matter for the blog

- Google and Meta reject most ads that mention ayahuasca or psilocybin. Growth comes from search content, reviews,
  referrals and partnerships. The blog is a main channel for profiles 1, 3, 4 and 6; supporting for 2, 5 and 7.
- Five cornerstone guides suggested: SSRIs and ayahuasca; Mexico vs Peru; psilocybin vs ayahuasca; how our screening
  works; what to expect in your first ceremony. One per profile, each ending in a call CTA.
