# Research: difficult-ayahuasca-ceremony

Profile: Healing Seeker. Keyword: "bad ayahuasca experience" / "mala experiencia con ayahuasca".
Searcher question: I'm afraid of having a terrible night. What happens if it gets hard, and can a hard night mean something?

## Sources

### Books

1. **Spiritual Emergency: When Personal Transformation Becomes a Crisis**. Stanislav Grof and Christina Grof (eds.), Tarcher, 1989. https://www.penguin.com.au/books/spiritual-emergency-9780874775389 (book, publisher page)
   - Publisher description: spiritual emergencies are "crises when the process of growth and change becomes chaotic and overwhelming."
   - Argues conventional psychiatry often fails to tell them apart from illness; reframes them as "transformative breakthroughs" which, handled supportively, can lead to new ways of living.
   - "the promise of spiritual emergence and renewal" can be found within the crisis.

2. **How to Change Your Mind**. Michael Pollan, Penguin Press, 2018. https://www.penguin.com.au/books/how-to-change-your-mind-9780141985138 (book, publisher page)
   - Book on the history and new science of psychedelics; Pollan tried them himself with guides.
   - Used for context only; the "flight instructions" quote comes from his NPR interview and Talks at Google talk about the book.

3. **Dark Night of the Soul / Noche oscura**. St. John of the Cross (San Juan de la Cruz, 1542 to 1591). EN: CCEL https://www.ccel.org/ccel/john_cross/dark_night.html ; ES poem text: Biblioteca Antológica https://www.biblioteca-antologica.org/es/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/CRUZ-Noche-oscura.pdf (book / classic text)
   - CCEL: the book traces the soul's journey, "the 'dark night'," to the "divine union of the love of God"; describes the night as excruciating.
   - Poem subtitle: songs of the soul that rejoices at having reached union with God "por el camino de la negación espiritual".
   - Poem calls the night "dichosa" and says "¡Oh noche que me guiaste!" (O night that guided me). Public domain.

### Research

10. **Carbonaro TM et al. (2016). Survey study of challenging experiences after ingesting psilocybin mushrooms.** Journal of Psychopharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881116662634 (online survey, n=1,993, read on PMC5551678)
    - About their single most difficult psilocybin experience: 39% rated it among the five most challenging experiences of their lives.
    - 34% rated it among the five most personally meaningful; 76% reported increased well-being or life satisfaction attributed to it.
    - 11% put self or others at risk of physical harm; 2.7% received medical help. Psilocybin, mostly unsupervised settings, not ayahuasca.

11. **Bouso JC et al. (2022). Adverse effects of ayahuasca: Results from the Global Ayahuasca Survey.** PLOS Global Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0000438 (survey, n=10,836)
    - 55.9% reported adverse mental health effects in the weeks or months after; around 88% (87.6%) considered them part of a positive process of growth or integration.
    - 62.0% reported vomiting/nausea; 11.9% needed professional support; 2.3% needed medical attention for physical effects.

12. **Andión O et al. (2025). A new insight into ayahuasca's adverse effects: reanalysis... Global Ayahuasca Survey.** PLOS Mental Health. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmen.0000097 (reanalysis, n=5,400, cross-sectional)
    - Extreme fear during ceremonies was the strongest predictor of adverse states afterwards.
    - Higher spiritual significance linked to better mental health; context shapes outcomes; people with depression history need extra support.
    - Cannot establish causation; self-reported, self-selected sample.

13. **Gómez-Sousa M et al. (2021). Acute psychological adverse reactions in first-time ritual ayahuasca users: a prospective case series.** Journal of Clinical Psychopharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1097/jcp.0000000000001343 (case series; read summary on ICEERS https://www.iceers.org/en/studies/adverse-reactions-in-first-time-ritual-ayahuasca-users/)
    - 7 of 40 first-time participants had intense challenging psychological effects.
    - At one month, 2 of 7 no longer had psychiatric symptoms and 2 had considerably reduced symptoms, holding at six months; 2 had higher scores.
    - Authors: poor setting, guiding and screening contributed to some reactions. Small sample.
