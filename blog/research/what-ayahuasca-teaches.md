## Sources (all opened and read)

### Books
1. **Jeremy Narby (1998; paperback 1999). The Cosmic Serpent: DNA and the Origins of Knowledge.** Tarcher / Penguin
   Random House, https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/349746/the-cosmic-serpent-by-jeremy-narby/9780874779646
   Publisher description: through research in the Amazon, Narby asks how shamans use visionary plants to "tap into
   knowledge and insights"; draws on indigenous knowledge, visionary experience and biology. Used only to say the book
   asks how Amazonian healers say they gain knowledge from plants.
2. **Jeremy Narby and Rafael Chanchari Pizuri (2021). Plant Teachers: Ayahuasca, Tobacco, and the Pursuit of
   Knowledge.** New World Library, https://newworldlibrary.com/product/plant-teachers
   Co-written with a Shawi elder and traditional healer from the Peruvian Amazon. A dialogue between Amazonian
   knowledge and science; describes the belief that some plants have an "owner" or "mother" one can learn from.
3. **Stephan V. Beyer (2009). Singing to the Plants: A Guide to Mestizo Shamanism in the Upper Amazon.** University of
   New Mexico Press. Record: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15468476W
   Describes mestizo shamanism, what happens in ayahuasca healing ceremonies and how apprentice shamans relate to
   plant spirits.

### Talks / interviews
4. **Gabor Maté on The Tim Ferriss Show #298 (2018). "Dr. Gabor Maté: Trauma, Addiction, Ayahuasca, and More."**
   YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H9B5mYfBPlY ; transcript read at
   https://tim.blog/2018/06/04/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-dr-gabor-mate/
   Physician and author on addiction and trauma. First ceremony: "I got in touch with such profound love that I had
   never consciously experienced before." Saw "all of the ways that I had closed my heart against love"; linked it to
   his mother giving him to a stranger at one year old to save his life. "I wish I could say that, after that
   experience, I became a loving husband and a loving human being. I didn't." "Never the less, it opened a door for
   me." Had "no induction... no processing afterwards". With adept practitioners it can be "like a super highway to
   self-awareness. Not in isolation"; "it has to be integrated"; "people don't come to it cold."
5. **Jeremy Narby (2019). "My life as a white vampire: Gringos, Amazonians and the antidote of reciprocity."** World
   Ayahuasca Conference, ICEERS, YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jAGdBhi1pZw (transcript read).
   Anthropologist, lived two years with Ashaninka people. Indigenous Amazonians "tend to say that those visuals are not
   the essential part there's the purge of course there are the revelations the understanding the ideas". Reciprocity
   is "an antidote"; suggests donating to an organization that backs indigenous Amazonian initiatives each time you
   drink. Quotes Shawi elder Rafael Chanchari Pizuri on a historical debt.

### Media / experts
8. **Adèle Lafrance (14 Dec 2016). "Ayahuasca and the Power of Forgiveness." Chacruna.**
   https://chacruna.net/ayahuasca-and-the-power-of-forgiveness/ (full text read). Clinical psychologist. Decisional
   forgiveness (a choice to respond peacefully) vs emotional forgiveness ("the replacement of negative, unforgiving
   emotions... with feelings of compassion"); "Emotional forgiveness involves emotional transformation". Her team's
   interviews: a number of participants described "emotional forgiveness" in child-parent, sibling and former romantic
   relationships; forgiveness of others and of themselves; "the 'how' of this process remains unclear".
9. **Rob La Frenais (13 Apr 2021). "Plant teachers, radical social distancing and falling skies: interview with Jeremy
   Narby." Makery.** https://www.makery.info/en/2021/04/13/english-plant-teachers-radical-social-distancing-and-falling-skies-interview-with-jeremy-narby/
   Amazonian peoples "consider them as teachers"; people who ingest them "can get knowledge, and gain insights and
   understandings about their current interests"; ayahuasca "has a knack for suggesting solutions to those who know
   how to ask pertinent questions"; "Just how this works is still a question of debate."
10. **Travis Lupick (3 Jun 2017). "Dr. Gabor Maté recounts experiences with ayahuasca for forthcoming documentary The
    Path of the Shaman." The Georgia Straight.** https://www.straight.com/node/918656
    Maté first tried ayahuasca in 2008 with a visiting Peruvian shaman; it "put me in touch with a deep love that I both
    wanted and had been running away from all my life."

### Research
11. **Luna, L. E. (1984). The concept of plants as teachers among four mestizo shamans of Iquitos, northeastern Peru.
    Journal of Ethnopharmacology 11(2):135-56.** https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-8741(84)90036-9 (PMID 6492831, abstract
    read). Vegetalistas use plants called "doctores or plant teachers"; if they keep isolation and a prescribed diet,
    the plants "teach" them how to diagnose and cure, perform shamanic tasks (through icaros) and use medicinal plants.
    Four shamans interviewed.
12. **Kavenská, V. and Simonová, H. (2015). Ayahuasca Tourism: Participants in Shamanic Rituals and their Personality
    Styles, Motivation, Benefits and Risks. Journal of Psychoactive Drugs 47(5):351-9.**
    https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2015.1094590 (PMID 26514589, abstract read). N=77 people who travelled to South
    America to drink ayahuasca. Motivations: curiosity, mental health, need for self-knowledge, spiritual development,
    finding direction in life. Most frequent benefits: self-knowledge, change in the way one relates to oneself,
    spiritual development, improved interpersonal relations, overcoming problems, a new perspective on life. Risks named:
    lack of trust in the shaman or organizer, inaccurate information, dangerous situations. Self-report survey.
