## Sources (all opened and read)

### Books
1. **William James (1902). The Varieties of Religious Experience.** Project Gutenberg,
   https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/621 (text read at https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621-images.html).
   Lectures XVI-XVII on mysticism: four marks. Ineffability ("defies expression... must be directly experienced") and
   noetic quality ("states of insight into depths of truth unplumbed by the discursive intellect... a curious sense of
   authority for after-time"); also transiency and passivity.
2. **Benny Shanon (2002). The Antipodes of the Mind: Charting the Phenomenology of the Ayahuasca Experience.**
   Oxford University Press, https://academic.oup.com/book/47914. Cognitive psychologist (Hebrew University). Abstract:
   "a pioneering cognitive psychological study of Ayahuasca"; based on his own sessions and interviews with indigenous
   people, shamans, members of ayahuasca religions and travellers. Chapters on consciousness, meaning, light, insights.
3. **Luis Eduardo Luna (1986). Vegetalismo: Shamanism among the Mestizo Population of the Peruvian Amazon.**
   Almqvist & Wiksell International, Stockholm studies in comparative religion 27. Record:
   https://openlibrary.org/books/OL2328661M/Vegetalismo. His doctoral dissertation (he says so in the HDS keynote).
4. **Michael Pollan (2018). How to Change Your Mind.** Author's page https://michaelpollan.com/books/how-to-change-your-mind/.
   Subtitle names consciousness and transcendence; first-person account plus research.

### Talks / conferences
5. **Luis Eduardo Luna, "Plant Animism in Western Amazonia" keynote, Psychedelic Intersections 2024, Harvard Divinity
   School (17 Feb 2024).** HDS Center for the Study of World Religions, YouTube, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mSEerfVMs9s
   (transcript read). Credible: anthropologist, ayahuasca research since the 1970s, Harvard Botanical Museum associate.
   Says: his teacher Don Emilio Andrade Gómez said ayahuasca is a "doctor", as is tobacco; "the idea of plant teachers";
   for Don Emilio "health and wisdom are two sides of the same coin"; the visions "are not so important". Published the
   plant-teacher concept in 1984; Vegetalismo was his dissertation. Host Charles Stang (director, CSWR) in his welcome:
   psychedelics are "one path... not a highway, but a web of paths".
6. **Michael Pollan, "Can Psychedelics Induce Mystical Experiences?" The Royal Institution, YouTube (2020).**
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40Os_t0LG94 (transcript read). Cites Griffiths 2006 title on mystical-type
   experiences "having substantial and sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance". Guides' advice "to
   surrender... not to resist". Tells of Dinah Bazer, an atheist cancer survivor in a psilocybin trial, who felt
   "bathed in God's love" and said "we don't have any word big enough". (Psilocybin, not ayahuasca: used only to
   illustrate ineffability and surrender.)
7. **Stanislav Grof, "Psychedelics and the spiritual path", Science and Nonduality, YouTube (2015).**
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n6yiMA2JXnY (transcript read). Psychiatrist, decades of research on non-ordinary
   states. Psychedelics are "catalysts"; going to the roots, "the spiritual dimension in the psyche opens"; it is "a
   direct experience... has nothing to do with belief". Retells Jung's "I know" answer about God.

### Media / experts
10. **Luis Eduardo Luna (2010). "Ayahuasca: Shamanism Shared Across Cultures." Cultural Survival Quarterly 27-2.**
    https://www.culturalsurvival.org/publications/cultural-survival-quarterly/ayahuasca-shamanism-shared-across-cultures
    Vegetalistas: mestizo practitioners, mostly in Peru, learned from plants they call "doctores, or plant teachers";
    the ayahuasqueros he met considered themselves Christians. Santo Daime: Raimundo Irineu Serra (1892-1971), Rio
    Branco, Acre, ayahuasca as central sacrament. Barquinha: Daniel Pereira de Matos. União do Vegetal: created in 1961
    by José Gabriel da Costa, Porto Velho. Blend of Amazonian, Afro-Brazilian, Catholic and European esoteric elements.

### Research
11. **Kjellgren, A., Eriksson, A. and Norlander, T. (2009). Experiences of encounters with ayahuasca: "the vine of the
    soul". Journal of Psychoactive Drugs 41(4).** https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2009.10399767 (full text read).
    Qualitative, 25 Northern Europeans, anonymous written accounts. Six themes ("transcendental circle"): motivation,
    frightening constricted state, sudden transformation, limitless expansive states, reflection, changed worldview and
    new orientation to life. Plant beings "functioned as teachers"; insight that "all is one"; "maybe love just is!";
    "I feel deeper contact with everything, from within in some way"; new worldview felt like something "previously
    known but had somehow been forgotten". Small sample, self-selected.
12. **Bouso, J. C. et al. (2012). Personality, psychopathology, life attitudes and neuropsychological performance among
    ritual users of ayahuasca: a longitudinal study. PLoS ONE 7(8): e42421.** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0042421
    127 long-term church users (Santo Daime CEFLURIS and Barquinha) vs 115 controls from other religions; follow-up
    one year later. Users higher on self-transcendence and on all nine Spiritual Orientation Inventory components at
    both points; Purpose in Life higher at baseline only. Observational; self-selection and self-report limits.
