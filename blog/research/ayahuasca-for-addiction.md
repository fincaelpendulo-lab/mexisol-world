## Sources

### Books

1. **C.G. Jung Letters, Volume 2: 1951-1961.** C.G. Jung, ed. Gerhard Adler. Princeton University Press (Bollingen), 1976.
   https://press.princeton.edu/isbn/9780691097244 (book). Type: book.
   The volume that collects Jung's letters up to shortly before his death, including the 30 January 1961 reply to Bill W.
   Text of the letter read on the World Community for Christian Meditation page (next entry).
2. **Carl Jung letter to Bill W.** Transcription published by the World Community for Christian Meditation (WCCM), posted 2018.
   https://wccm.org/outreach-areas/addiction-recovery/carl-jung-letter-to-bill-w. Type: primary text (letter), via a credible org.
   Dated January 30, 1961, to "Mr. William G. Wilson, Alcoholics Anonymous". Exact lines: "His craving for alcohol was the
   equivalent on a low level of the spiritual thirst of our being for wholeness"; "the union with God"; the experience can come
   "by an act of grace or through a personal and honest contact with friends, or through a higher education of the mind";
   "Alcohol in Latin is 'spiritus'"; "The helpful formula therefore is: spiritus contra spiritum." Refers to Roland H.
3. **In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts: Close Encounters with Addiction.** Gabor Maté. North Atlantic Books (US ed. 2010; Knopf
   Canada 2008/2009). https://www.northatlanticbooks.com/shop/in-the-realm-of-hungry-ghosts/. Type: book.
   Publisher: the book "traces the root causes of addiction to childhood trauma"; stresses "the importance of self-compassion
   in recovery".
4. **Atomic Habits.** James Clear. Avery, 2018. https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits. Type: book.
   Line on the author's page: "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

### Research

10. **Thomas G, Lucas P, Capler NR, Tupper KW, Martin G (2013).** Ayahuasca-assisted therapy for addiction: results from a
    preliminary observational study in Canada. Current Drug Abuse Reviews 6(1):30-42. https://doi.org/10.2174/15733998113099990003
    (PMID 23627784). Type: observational study, n=12, no control group.
    "Working with Addiction and Stress" retreats in a rural First Nations community in BC "combined four days of group counselling
    with two expert-led ayahuasca ceremonies". Six-month follow-up. Significant improvements in hopefulness, empowerment,
    mindfulness, quality of life. Self-reported alcohol, tobacco and cocaine use declined; only the drop in problematic cocaine
    use was statistically significant; cannabis and opiate use did not change.
11. **Argento E, Capler R, Thomas G, Lucas P, Tupper KW (2019).** Exploring ayahuasca-assisted therapy for addiction: a qualitative
    analysis of preliminary findings among an Indigenous community in Canada. Drug and Alcohol Review 38(7):781-789.
    https://doi.org/10.1111/dar.12985 (PMID 31489731). Type: qualitative study, n=11.
    Interviews at six months: all 11 reported reduced use and cravings; 8 reported stopping at least one substance. Increased
    connectedness with self, others and nature/spirit was "a key element associated with reduced substance use and cravings".
12. **Loizaga-Velder A, Verres R (2014).** Therapeutic effects of ritual ayahuasca use in the treatment of substance dependence:
    qualitative results. Journal of Psychoactive Drugs 46(1):63-72. https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2013.873157 (PMID 24830187).
    Type: qualitative interview study. 13 therapists, 2 expert researchers, 14 people who had ayahuasca-assisted addiction
    therapy in South America. In carefully structured settings ayahuasca may support recovery and relapse prevention; outcomes
    depend on several variables.
13. **Fábregas JM, González D, Fondevila S, et al. (2010).** Assessment of addiction severity among ritual users of ayahuasca.
    Drug and Alcohol Dependence 111(3):257-261. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2010.03.024 (PMID 20554400).
    Type: cross-sectional with one-year follow-up. Brazil: 56 jungle-based users vs 56 rural controls; 71 urban users vs 59 urban
    controls. Users scored lower on ASI Alcohol Use and Psychiatric Status in both studies; past illicit drug use had stopped
    (except cannabis in study 1).
14. **Bogenschutz MP, Ross S, Bhatt S, et al. (2022).** Percentage of heavy drinking days following psilocybin-assisted
    psychotherapy vs placebo in the treatment of adult patients with alcohol use disorder. JAMA Psychiatry 79(10):953-962.
    https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.2096. Type: double-blind RCT, n=95, active placebo diphenhydramine.
    Heavy drinking days over 32 weeks: 9.7% psilocybin vs 23.6% placebo. Psilocybin + 12 weeks psychotherapy.
15. **Brunt TM (2026).** Rare but relevant: ibogaine and cardiovascular complications, prolonged QT interval and ventricular
    arrhythmias. Addiction 121(6):1616-1621. https://doi.org/10.1111/add.70319. Type: review.
    Observational, open-label and limited RCT evidence suggests ibogaine reduces craving and withdrawal in opioid- and
    cocaine-dependent people; risk of QTc prolongation and potentially fatal ventricular arrhythmias, case reports at
    therapeutic doses, including people without heart conditions.
