# Research: change-habits-after-retreat

Keyword: "how to change habits after a psychedelic experience" / "cómo cambiar hábitos después de un retiro".
Profile: Pattern Breaker. Publish date 2026-11-30. Spanish slug: cambiar-habitos-despues-de-un-retiro.

## Sources

### Books

1. **Atomic Habits: Tiny Changes, Remarkable Results.** James Clear. Avery (Penguin Random House), 2018.
   https://jamesclear.com/atomic-habits (author's book page). Also read the book excerpt (chapter 2) at
   https://jamesclear.com/identity-based-habits. Type: book.
   Page: "tiny, easy changes that deliver big results"; "get 1% better every day"; bad habits persist because of the
   wrong system. Chapter 2 excerpt: start from who you want to become, then prove it with small wins.
   Quote used: "In order to believe in a new identity, we have to prove it to ourselves."

2. **In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts: Close Encounters with Addiction.** Gabor Maté. North Atlantic Books, 2010
   (US edition; first published in Canada 2008). https://www.northatlanticbooks.com/shop/in-the-realm-of-hungry-ghosts/
   and author page https://drgabormate.com/book/in-the-realm-of-hungry-ghosts/ Type: book.
   Addiction as a continuum across society, rooted in personal history and often childhood pain; people use
   substances or behaviours to soothe "deep-seated fears and discomforts". Compassionate self-understanding as a first step.

### Talks and interviews

3. **A simple way to break a bad habit.** Judson Brewer, TEDMED 2015. Psychiatrist who studies mindfulness and addiction.
   https://www.ted.com/talks/judson_brewer_a_simple_way_to_break_a_bad_habit Type: talk.
   Habits run on reward-based learning: "Trigger, behavior, reward", laid down as context-dependent memory, and later
   used to soothe emotions. A smoker told to smoke with curiosity found it "smells like stinky cheese and tastes like
   chemicals"; she moved "from knowing in her head ... to knowing it in her bones". In one of his studies mindfulness
   training was "twice as good as gold standard therapy" at helping people quit smoking.

4. **Dr. Gabor Maté: New Paradigms, Ayahuasca, and Redefining Addiction.** The Tim Ferriss Show #298, 20 Feb 2018.
   https://tim.blog/2018/02/20/gabor-mate/ ; transcript https://tim.blog/2018/06/04/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-dr-gabor-mate/
   Type: long-form podcast. Maté (physician, addiction author) says the question is not "why the addiction but why the
   pain". People he has seen benefit did so "so long as the proper integration is done afterwards". His own first
   ceremony had "no processing afterwards". Show notes: "The importance of integrating by sharing our powerful experiences with others."

### Media and creators

7. **Good Habits, Bad Habits: A Conversation with Wendy Wood.** Michaela Barnett, Behavioral Scientist, 14 Oct 2019.
   https://behavioralscientist.org/?p=21433 Type: expert interview (author of Good Habits, Bad Habits, FSG 2019).
   "Many people actually confuse habit and self-control." "We are all dependent on the context in which we live."
   Moves can disrupt habits if they change cues and can open a window for new ones; in her study of transfer
   students, habits survived when the new setting kept the cues and broke when key cues were missing.

8. **How to form a habit.** BPS Research Digest (British Psychological Society), 6 Oct 2010, on Lally et al. 2010.
   https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit Type: credible science media.
   96 recruited, 82 completed; daily behaviour tied to a cue; "The average time to reach maximum automaticity was 66
   days", from 18 to a predicted 254 days; "a single missed day had little impact on later automaticity gains".

9. **The Art of Integration: How to Support Ayahuasca Experiences.** Igor Domsac, ICEERS, 5 Dec 2025.
   https://www.iceers.org/art-integration-support-ayahuasca-experiences/ Type: expert organization article.
   "the experience does not end when the ceremony concludes, but when the community receives the participant again";
   integration is social as well as individual.

### Research

10. Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C., Potts, H. and Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: modelling habit formation in
    the real world. European Journal of Social Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674 (numbers via BPS, above).
11. Johnson, M. W., Garcia-Romeu, A., Cosimano, M. P. and Griffiths, R. R. (2014). Pilot study of the 5-HT2AR agonist
    psilocybin in the treatment of tobacco addiction. J Psychopharmacol. https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881114548296
    Open-label pilot, 15 smokers (mean 6 earlier quit attempts), psilocybin within a structured 15-week cessation
    programme (CBT). 12 of 15 (80%) abstinent at 6 months. Open label: no firm efficacy conclusion.
12. Johnson, M. W., Garcia-Romeu, A. and Griffiths, R. R. (2017). Long-term follow-up of psilocybin-facilitated smoking
    cessation. Am J Drug Alcohol Abuse. https://doi.org/10.3109/00952990.2016.1170135
    10 of 15 (67%) abstinent at 12 months; 9 (60%) at long-term follow-up (mean 30 months). 13 rated the sessions among
    the five most meaningful experiences of their lives.
13. Noorani, T., Garcia-Romeu, A., Swift, T. C., Griffiths, R. R. and Johnson, M. W. (2018). Psychedelic therapy for
    smoking cessation: qualitative analysis of participant accounts. J Psychopharmacol.
    https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881118780612 Interviews with 12 of 15, about 30 months after. Vivid insights into
    self-identity and reasons for smoking; awe and curiosity persisted; preparatory counselling, rapport with the team
    and momentum were "vital additional factors".
14. Cowley-Court, T., Chenhall, R., Sarris, J., Bouso, J. C., Tófoli, L. F., Opaleye, E. S., Schubert, V. and Perkins,
    D. (2023). Life after ayahuasca: a qualitative analysis of the psychedelic integration experiences of 1630
    ayahuasca drinkers from a global survey. Psychoactives. https://doi.org/10.3390/psychoactives2020014
    Survey 2017-2019, 1,630 drinkers. Integration can be challenging and take considerable time; helpful: like-minded
    community, ongoing yoga, meditation, journaling; challenges: feeling disconnected, going back to "old life".
15. Evens, R., Uyar, A. and Majić, T. (2025, online; print 2026). Managing psychological challenges in the subacute ("afterglow") window
    of psychedelic drug effects. Current Topics in Behavioral Neurosciences. https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2025_612
    Afterglow: elevated mood, well-being, openness, sometimes clarity and meaning. Not everyone has it; some struggle to
    integrate. Integration often individual or through peers and social support; professional care when severe.
16. Majić, T. et al. (2025). The Afterglow Inventory (AGI). J Psychopharmacol. https://doi.org/10.1177/02698811251326937
    Calls the afterglow "temporary"; survey of 1,323 people who had taken a psychedelic within the past 4 weeks; five
    factors incl. vitality, relationships, relationship to nature.

## Claims and sources

- Afterglow = temporary period of better mood, openness, sometimes clarity; not everyone has it: 15, 16.
- Integration can take considerable time; going back to "old life" is a common challenge; community, yoga,
  meditation, journaling help: 14.
- Integration is social: 9; Maté on sharing and integration afterwards: 4.
- Habits run on trigger, behaviour, reward and context-dependent memory: 3. Context and cues, moves, habit vs
  self-control: 7.
- Addiction soothes pain; "why the pain": 2, 4.
- 66 days average, 18 to 254, a missed day matters little: 8, 10.
- Small changes and identity: 1.
- Psilocybin smoking pilot numbers, structured programme, small open-label: 11, 12. Insights into identity and the
  support around it: 13.
- Curiosity, knowing in the bones; mindfulness twice as good in one study: 3.
- Engine and transmission; doing over talking: 5.
- Guest quotes: 6.
- MexiSol facts: group and one-on-one integration during the week (src/pages/ayahuasca-retreat.astro); "We stay in
  touch after the retreat, and the group usually does too" (src/pages/prepare.astro); integration coach is a woman,
  screening content, no good or bad experiences (blog/house-facts.md); dependence on alcohol, benzodiazepines or
  opioids is listed as a reason a retreat may not be safe (src/pages/medical.astro, prepare.astro).
