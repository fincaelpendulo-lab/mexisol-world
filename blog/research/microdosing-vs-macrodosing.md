### Research

12. **Szigeti B. et al. (2021). Self-blinding citizen science to explore psychedelic microdosing. eLife 10:e62878.**
    https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.62878 Type: placebo-controlled citizen-science study. 191 completers who already
    microdosed made their own placebo capsules for 4 weeks. Both groups improved; no significant difference; small
    differences explained by breaking blind. "The findings suggest that anecdotal benefits of microdosing can be
    explained by the placebo effect." Limits: self-sourced drug, not clinical.

13. **Cavanna F. et al. (2022). Microdosing with psilocybin mushrooms: a double-blind placebo-controlled study.
    Translational Psychiatry 12:307.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-022-02039-0 Type: double-blind RCT, 34 people
    starting psilocybin-mushroom microdosing. Acute effects stronger on active dose only among those who guessed
    their condition; no benefit on well-being, creativity or cognition; "expectation underlies at least some of the
    anecdotal benefits." Healthy volunteers, small sample.

14. **Griffiths R. R. et al. (2006). Psilocybin can occasion mystical-type experiences having substantial and
    sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance. Psychopharmacology 187:268-283.**
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5 Type: double-blind study, 36 hallucinogen-naive adults, supportive
    setting with monitors. At 2 months, "substantial personal meaning and spiritual significance".

15. **Griffiths R. R. et al. (2008). Mystical-type experiences occasioned by psilocybin mediate the attribution of
    personal meaning and spiritual significance 14 months later. Journal of Psychopharmacology 22(6):621-632.**
    https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881108094300 Type: follow-up. 14 months: 58% among five most personally meaningful
    experiences of life; 67% among five most spiritually significant; 64% increased well-being or life satisfaction.
    Also confirmed on https://www.hopkinspsychedelic.org/history
