# Research: ayahuasca-and-grief

Profile: Crossroads. Keyword: "ayahuasca and grief" / "ayahuasca y duelo". Publish date 2026-11-17.
All sources opened and read on 2026-11-09.

## Sources

### Research

1. **Potential Use of Ayahuasca in Grief Therapy.** González D, Carvalho M, Cantillo J, Aixalá M, Farré M.
   OMEGA, Journal of Death and Dying 79(3):260-285 (online 2017, issue 2019). https://doi.org/10.1177/0030222817710879 (PMID 28556759). Type: cross-sectional comparison + qualitative.
   Compared 30 bereaved people who had taken ayahuasca with 30 who attended peer-support groups. Ayahuasca group had
   lower scores on the Present Feelings scale of the Texas Revised Inventory of Grief. Open answers described
   emotional release, biographical memories and experiences of contact with the deceased. Preliminary.

2. **Therapeutic potential of ayahuasca in grief: a prospective, observational study.** González D, Cantillo J,
   Pérez I, Farré M, Feilding A, Obiols JE, Bouso JC. Psychopharmacology 237(4):1171-1182 (2020).
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-019-05446-2 (PMID 31938878, PMC7113212). Type: prospective observational, no control.
   50 bereaved people at baseline before Shipibo ceremonies at a center in Peru (average 6.62 ceremonies, range 4 to 9);
   39 at 15 days, 31 at 3 months, 29 at 6 months, 27 at 12 months. Grief severity (TRIG) fell at every time point
   (d = 0.84 to 1.39). Less experiential avoidance (r = 0.55) and more decentering (r = -0.47) went with less grief.
   92.3% of those at T1 (n = 39) said ayahuasca had a positive effect on their grief. Limits stated: no placebo group,
   small sample, nine people had psychotherapy during follow-up.

3. **Ayahuasca-assisted meaning reconstruction therapy as an early resource for bereavement: a non-randomized
   clinical trial.** Soto-Angona O, Andión O, Sabucedo P, Neimeyer RA, ... González D. Scientific Reports 15:32158 (2025).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-13251-5 (PMID 40890317). Type: three-arm, non-randomized, open-label trial.
   84 adults with severe grief within 12 months of losing a first-degree relative: ayahuasca + meaning reconstruction
   therapy (28), therapy alone (28), no treatment (28). All three groups improved, including no treatment
   (d = 0.74); the ayahuasca group improved most (d = 2.44). No serious adverse events. Authors: preliminary,
   needs larger randomized trials.

### Books

4. **The Wild Edge of Sorrow: Rituals of Renewal and the Sacred Work of Grief.** Francis Weller. North Atlantic Books,
   2015. https://www.northatlanticbooks.com/shop/the-wild-edge-of-sorrow/
   Psychotherapist; ritual-based approach to grief. Five gates of grief; the first: "everything we love, we will lose".
   Others: places that have not known love, sorrows of the world, what we expected and did not receive, ancestral grief.

5. **Die Wise: A Manifesto for Sanity and Soul.** Stephen Jenkinson. North Atlantic Books, 2015.
   https://www.northatlanticbooks.com/shop/die-wise/
   Draws on about two decades with dying people and their families; dying well and carrying our dead as
   responsibilities of a community, not coping techniques.

### Talks, interviews, documentary

6. **Francis Weller: Our Apprenticeship with Sorrow.** Insights at the Edge, host Tami Simon, Sounds True, 25 Feb 2025.
   https://resources2.soundstrue.com/podcast/francis-weller-our-apprenticeship-with-sorrow/ (transcript read).
   Weller: "We're not taught how to work with grief, how to accompany it, how to befriend it." "The main thing is to
   not think of it in isolation." "ritual doesn't have to be complicated."

7. **How Engaging with Loss Can Help Us Live More Fully with Stephen Jenkinson | TGS 190.** The Great Simplification,
   host Nate Hagens, YouTube, 2025 (recorded 12 June 2025). https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XgWpq66LuWY
   (transcript PDF read). Jenkinson: "The skillfulness is the mourning that you don't try to accelerate through it";
   "Grief is your companion now, not your adversary." Credible: former head of a palliative care counselling team.

8. **Griefwalker.** Dir. Tim Wilson, National Film Board of Canada, 2008. https://www.nfb.ca/film/griefwalker/
   Documentary on Stephen Jenkinson, who led a palliative care counselling team at Mount Sinai Hospital in Toronto
   and attended the deathbeds of over 1,000 people.

### Testimonials

9. **Prince Harry says he's used psychedelics to help cope with grief.** Kolbe Nelson, CBS News (60 Minutes), 9 Jan 2023.
   https://www.cbsnews.com/news/prince-harry-psychedelics-60-minutes-2023-01-09/
   Cooper named ayahuasca and psilocybin; Harry: "I would never recommend people to do this recreationally";

   prove he missed his mother, "When in fact, all she wanted was for me to be happy."

10. **How Tripping On Ayahuasca Helped Me Process My Mum's Murder.** Celia Peachey as told to Kate Johnson, VICE, 29 July 2015.
   https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-tripping-on-ayahuasca-helped-me-process-my-mums-murder-284/
   First-person: ceremonies at a retreat in Spain in a psychotherapeutic setting. "It was like something was
   breathing for me." "I have cleared my sadness and freed myself from a lot of grief." Written about two weeks after.

### Media / organizations / culture

11. **La ayahuasca ayuda a superar los procesos de duelo** (press release). ICEERS, January 2020.
   https://www.iceers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Ayahuasca-en-procesos-de-duelo-Nota-de-prensa.pdf
   Lead researcher Débora González: «Hemos podido constatar que la ayahuasca facilita la aceptación de la muerte de un
   ser querido». In the 2017 study a third of bereaved ayahuasca drinkers reported reencounters with the "presence",
   "soul" or "essence" of their loved one. Calls ayahuasca «liana de los muertos».

12. **Indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead.** UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, inscribed 2008.
   https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indigenous-festivity-dedicated-to-the-dead-00054 (ES: https://ich.unesco.org/es/RL/fiestas-indigenas-dedicadas-a-los-muertos-00054)
   Celebrates the "transitory return to Earth of deceased relatives and loved ones"; petals and candles from the
   cemetery to the home; favorite foods on the home altar and grave; blends pre-Hispanic and Catholic traditions.
