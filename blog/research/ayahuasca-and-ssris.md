# Research: ayahuasca-and-ssris

Keyword: "ayahuasca and SSRIs" / "ayahuasca y antidepresivos". Profile: Healing Seeker. Publish date 2026-11-07.

## Sources

### Academic and government health bodies

1. **Ayahuasca preparations and serotonin reuptake inhibitors: a potential combination for severe adverse interactions.**
   Callaway JC, Grob CS. Journal of Psychoactive Drugs, 1998, 30(4):367-9. https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.1998.10399712 (PMID 9924842). Type: short report / commentary (classic paper).
   The harmala alkaloids in ayahuasca are potent MAO inhibitors; combined with SSRIs they "may induce a serotonin
   syndrome with potentially grave outcome". Caution advised with certain pharmaceutical drugs. Little attention had
   been given to interactions. Read via PubMed record (E-utilities abstract).

2. **Serotonin toxicity of serotonergic psychedelics.** Malcolm B, Thomas K. Psychopharmacology, 2022, 239(6):1881-1891
   (online July 2021). https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-021-05876-x (PMID 34251464). Type: critical literature review.
   True serotonin toxicity typically occurs with overdose or when a drug that raises synaptic serotonin is combined
   with an MAOI. Serotonergic drugs without MAOIs are low risk with psychedelics that do not contain MAOIs. Signs
   needing urgent care: myoclonus, extreme/fluctuating vital signs, agitation or coma, muscle rigidity, high fever,
   seizures. Toxicity exists on a spectrum, serotonin syndrome is the most severe end.

3. **Predicting drug-drug interactions between ayahuasca alkaloids and SSRIs using physiologically based
   pharmacokinetic modeling.** Ribeiro GSG, ... dos Santos RG, ... Marcourakis T. Frontiers in Molecular Biosciences,
   2026, 13:1768402. https://doi.org/10.3389/fmolb.2026.1768402 (PMID 41788629). Type: computer modelling study (PBPK),
   models qualified on data from six volunteers given ayahuasca.
   Harmine is a reversible MAO-A inhibitor that lets DMT be active by mouth. Fluoxetine and paroxetine are strong
   CYP2D6 inhibitors; simulations showed they raised harmine exposure and moderately raised DMT, in acute and chronic
   dosing. Authors suggest a clinically relevant interaction. Simulation, not a trial.

4. **Tapering of SSRI treatment to mitigate withdrawal symptoms.** Horowitz MA, Taylor D. Lancet Psychiatry, 2019,
   6(6):538-546. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30850328/ (DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(19)30032-X). Type: Personal View / review with PET data analysis.
   All antidepressant classes are associated with withdrawal syndromes; SSRI withdrawal is often and can be severe,
   and can be mistaken for relapse. Guidelines recommend 2 to 4 week tapers, which show minimal benefit over abrupt
   stopping; tapers over months to much lower doses showed greater success. Suggest slow hyperbolic tapering.

5. **Incidence of antidepressant discontinuation symptoms: a systematic review and meta-analysis.** Henssler J, Schmidt
   Y, Schmidt U, Schwarzer G, Bschor T, Baethge C. Lancet Psychiatry, 2024, 11(7):526-535.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38851198/ (DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00133-0). Type: systematic review and meta-analysis.
   79 studies, 21,002 patients. At least one discontinuation symptom in 31% after stopping an antidepressant vs 17%
   after placebo. Severe symptoms 2.8% vs 0.6%. Adjusted for non-specific effects, about 15%, one in six to seven.
   Paroxetine, imipramine, venlafaxine/desvenlafaxine linked to more severe symptoms. Heterogeneity substantial.

6. **PROZAC (fluoxetine) prescribing information.** US FDA label, DailyMed (NIH National Library of Medicine), label
   updated January 2026. https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c88f33ed-6dfb-4c5e-bc01-d8e36dd97299
   Type: government drug label.
   Section 2.9: "at least 5 weeks should be allowed after stopping PROZAC before starting an MAOI". Section 12.3:
   fluoxetine half-life 1 to 3 days acute, 4 to 6 days chronic; norfluoxetine 4 to 16 days. Section 5.15: gradual
   reduction rather than abrupt cessation recommended whenever possible. Serotonin syndrome signs: mental status
   changes (agitation, hallucinations, delirium, coma), autonomic instability, neuromuscular symptoms, seizures, GI symptoms.

7. **ZOLOFT (sertraline) prescribing information.** US FDA label, DailyMed, July 2025.
   https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fda754f6-d0f3-4dce-a17a-927d64f912f7 Type: government drug label.
   Section 2.5: "at least 14 days must elapse after stopping ZOLOFT before starting an MAOI antidepressant". Section
   12.3: average terminal half-life about 26 hours. Section 2.6: reduce gradually rather than abruptly. Section 5.5
   discontinuation symptoms: nausea, sweating, dysphoric mood, irritability, agitation, dizziness, electric-shock-like
   sensations, tremor, anxiety, confusion, headache, lethargy, emotional lability, insomnia, hypomania, tinnitus, seizures.

8. **Stopping or coming off antidepressants.** NHS (UK National Health Service), last reviewed 12 June 2025.
   https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/talking-therapies-medicine-treatments/medicines-and-psychiatry/stopping-or-coming-off-antidepressants/
   Type: government health body.
   Do not stop suddenly or without talking to your doctor; agree a plan to reduce gradually, usually over several weeks
   or months. Symptoms: headaches, aches, nausea, sweating, palpitations, dizziness, sleep problems, low mood,
   restlessness, anxiety, confusion, "brain zaps". Some can feel like the condition returning. Usually start within a
   few days and last a few weeks; for some longer, more likely with long use or higher doses.

### News and long-form media

9. **People say they've faced withdrawals from SSRIs. They want recognition and research.** Emily Corwin (APM Reports),
   NPR All Things Considered, 27 June 2025.
   https://www.npr.org/2025/06/27/nx-s1-5445196/people-say-theyve-faced-withdrawals-from-ssris-they-want-recognition-and-research
   Type: public radio news. Tufts psychiatrist Nassir Ghaemi: severe short-term withdrawal more common than long-term
   effects; "the longer you take the drugs, the more likely it is you'll have problems going off them"; "doctors say
   don't stop antidepressants cold turkey. It's important to go slow." Little large-scale research exists.

10. **Weekly Dose: ayahuasca, a cautionary tale for tourists eager to try this shamanic brew.** Stephen Bright (Edith
    Cowan University), David Caldicott (ANU emergency medicine). The Conversation, 14 March 2017.
    https://theconversation.com/weekly-dose-ayahuasca-a-cautionary-tale-for-tourists-eager-to-try-this-shamanic-brew-73953
    Type: academic-written journalism with editorial standards. The vine contains reversible MAO inhibitors that let
    DMT reach the body; turning off these enzymes exposes the body to toxicological effects; "substantial numbers of
    drugs" including "medications for depression" could have "significant, even potentially lethal consequences".

11. **Uno de cada 6 pacientes que dejan de tomar antidepresivos tiene "síndrome de abstinencia", según un estudio.**
    Dennis Thompson (HealthDay), Infobae, 7 June 2024.
    https://www.infobae.com/salud/2024/06/07/1-de-cada-6-pacientes-que-dejan-de-tomar-antidepresivos-tiene-sindrome-de-abstinencia/
    Type: Spanish-language news (HealthDay wire). Reports Henssler 2024: 79 studies, 21,000+ patients, 31%, about 3%
    severe, 17% placebo, 15% (1 in 6) direct. Talk to your doctor before stopping. Used in the Spanish post.

### YouTube and creators

12. **Dr. Mark Horowitz: Antidepressant withdrawal effects and how to safely stop them.** Therapeutics Initiative
    (University of British Columbia), YouTube, uploaded May 2024 (webinar 8 May 2024), 52 minutes.
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRr09mvMEAU Credible: UBC's independent drug evidence group; speaker is lead author
    of the Maudsley Deprescribing Guidelines, NHS psychiatry research fellow, visiting lecturer King's College London.
    Covers risks of stopping too quickly, telling withdrawal from relapse, individualised hyperbolic tapering.

13. **The risks of mixing ayahuasca and SSRIs: 5 Questions for UCLA psychiatry professor Charles Grob.** Jane C. Hu,
    The Microdose (newsletter of the UC Berkeley Center for the Science of Psychedelics), 6 January 2025.
    https://themicrodose.substack.com/p/the-risks-of-mixing-ayahuasca-and Type: expert interview newsletter.
    Grob (co-author of the 1998 paper) describes three US cases on SSRIs who became very agitated, confused and sweaty
    during ayahuasca and returned to baseline afterwards; suspected serotonin syndrome; severe cases can involve
    seizures. Says SSRIs "can take up to 4 or 5 weeks" to fully leave the system; hesitant to advise stopping other
    meds such as blood pressure or heart rhythm drugs.
