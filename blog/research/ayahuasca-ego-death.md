# Research: ayahuasca-ego-death

Plan: blog/plans/2026-11.json, 2026-11-21, profile Returning Journeyer. Keyword "ayahuasca ego death" /
"muerte del ego ayahuasca". Plan note: do not promise ego death or present it as a goal; mention psychosis risk
(link /medical).

Correction to the plan's angle: the plan said the 2023 study linked death experiences to "less fear of death".
The paper itself found NO difference in death anxiety (contrary to the authors' hypothesis). What it did find was a
higher sense of death transcendence (Study 1) and more certainty that consciousness continues after death (Study 2).
The post says this.

### Media / experts
9. **Clancy Cavnar, Psy.D. (2017). "The Effects of Ayahuasca on the Ego." Chacruna, 20 June 2017.**
   https://chacruna.net/the-effects-of-ayahuasca-on-the-ego/ Ayahuasca acts on the ego as "malleable, able to
   contract it and expand it": contraction through remorse and facing failings; expansion through compassion and
   unity, which can also feed self-importance: "The hungry ego, once fed, becomes ravenous and marvels at itself."
   People with fragile ego structures such as psychoses may find ayahuasca "further disintegrate their ego strength".
10. **Sarah Russo and Igor Domsac (2022). "Plant Medicine and the Ego: A Process of Reintegration." ICEERS,
    11 Oct 2022.** https://www.iceers.org/plant-medicine-ego-reintegration/ (ES:
    https://www.iceers.org/es/noticias/medicina-vegetal-ego-reintegracion/). Interview with David Londoño,
    psychologist, coordinator of the ICEERS Support Center. "There is a difference between transcending and abandoning
    or losing the ego." "It is not about breaking or fighting with the ego, but rather building a healthy ego."
    Prolonged loss of the sense of self needs care. Inflation (narcissism, feeling special, "the center of the world")
    is a frequent risk he sees; preparation and guidance matter.
11. **Vladimir Hedrih (2024). "New study reveals how common ayahuasca-induced death experiences are..." PsyPost,
    22 Feb 2024.** https://www.psypost.org/new-study-reveals-how-common-ayahuasca-induced-death-experiences-are-and-their-link-to-personal-transformation/
    Summary of source 12: 67% and 49%; APDs did not influence death anxiety; self-report, no causation. (PsyPost
    gives Study 1 as 44 people; the paper says 54. We use the paper.)

### Research
12. **David, J., Bouso, J. C., Kohek, M., Ona, G., Tadmor, N., Arnon, T., Dor-Ziderman, Y. and Berkovich-Ohana, A.
    (2023). Ayahuasca-induced personal death experiences: prevalence, characteristics, and impact on attitudes toward
    death, life, and the environment. Frontiers in Psychiatry 14.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1287961 (full
    text read on frontiersin.org). Two cross-sectional studies. Study 1: 54 veteran users, 36 (66.7%) reported an APD.
    Study 2: online, Israeli users, 305 analysed, 150 (49.2%). APD described as "an overwhelmingly authentic and
    convincing sensation of acutely dying or being dead". APD group: higher death transcendence (Study 1), more
    certainty that consciousness continues after death, higher life fulfilment and problem-focused coping (Study 2).
    No difference in death anxiety. Not linked to demographics, personality or psychopathology (depression, anxiety,
    depersonalization). Limits: self-report, cross-sectional, no causality, mostly Israel, set and setting not
    controlled.
13. **Nour, M. M., Evans, L., Nutt, D. and Carhart-Harris, R. L. (2016). Ego-Dissolution and Psychedelics:
    Validation of the Ego-Dissolution Inventory (EDI). Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 10:269.**
    https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00269 (full text read). "The experience of a compromised sense of 'self',
    termed ego-dissolution, is a key feature of the psychedelic experience." Online survey, 691 people, 1,828 drug
    experiences. EDI items include "I experienced a decrease in my sense of self-importance" and "I felt far less
    absorbed by my own issues and concerns". A separate ego-inflation scale was linked with cocaine, not psychedelics
    or alcohol. Links disturbed self-experience to psychosis as an open research question. Retrospective, self-report.
