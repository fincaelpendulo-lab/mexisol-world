## Sources (all opened and read)

### Books

1. **Judson Brewer, *The Craving Mind: From Cigarettes to Smartphones to Love* (Yale University Press, 2017; paperback 2018).**
   Excerpt "Addicted to Distraction" on Yale University Press's site: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/?p=8974 (type: book excerpt).
   Explains how cues paired with reward set off craving before the reward arrives. Quote used: "These are simply
   environmental cues that we have learned to pair with rewarding behavior." Brewer: psychiatrist, addiction and
   mindfulness researcher (UMass Center for Mindfulness, adjunct at Yale, per the page).

2. **Annie Grace, *This Naked Mind* (Avery / Penguin Random House; ebook 2015, paperback 2018).**
   https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/577190/this-naked-mind-by-annie-grace/ (type: book page).
   Description says most people "assume giving it up will involve deprivation and misery"; the book offers a different
   view of alcohol and drinking culture. Quote used (from description): "assume giving it up will involve deprivation and misery".

3. **Ram Dass and Rameshwar Das, *Being Ram Dass* (Sounds True, ©2020, published 2021).** Companion site:
   https://beingramdass.soundstrue.com (type: book site). Shows the quote "If you want to see how you are doing on your
   spiritual path, go spend a week with your parents." attributed to Ram Dass. (The popular "enlightened / family"
   variant has no primary source; we use only the wording on the book's own site.)

4. **C. G. Jung, letter to Bill W., 30 January 1961**, WCCM: https://wccm.org/outreach-areas/addiction-recovery/carl-jung-letter-to-bill-w
   (type: primary document / letter). "the spiritual thirst of our being for wholeness"; "The helpful formula therefore is:
   spiritus contra spiritum."

### Research and health

12. **Larimer, Palmer and Marlatt (1999). Relapse prevention: an overview of Marlatt's cognitive-behavioral model. Alcohol Research & Health, 23(2).**
    PMID 10890810 / PMC6760427. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10890810/ (type: review, NIAAA journal).
    Negative emotions "associated with the highest rate of relapse"; interpersonal conflict "(e.g., an argument with a family member)";
    with negative states, triggers for more than half of relapse episodes in Marlatt's 1996 analysis; social pressure, including being
    around people who drink, "more than 20 percent"; celebrations, alcohol cues, testing willpower also high-risk. "Abstinence violation
    effect": people who blame a lapse on personal failure feel guilt and drink more; "the progression from lapse to relapse is not inevitable."
    Example thought: "I owe myself a drink".

13. **Bowen and Marlatt (2009). Surfing the urge: brief mindfulness-based intervention for college student smokers. Psychology of Addictive Behaviors.**
    https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017127 (type: randomized lab study). 123 undergraduate smokers; urges did not differ, but the mindfulness
    group smoked significantly fewer cigarettes over 7 days. "may not initially reduce urges ... but may change the response to urges." Smoking, small, short follow-up.

14. **Cooke, McEwan and Norman (2022, online). The effect of forming implementation intentions on alcohol consumption: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Drug and Alcohol Review.**
    https://doi.org/10.1111/dar.13553 (type: meta-analysis, 16 studies). If-then plans gave a small reduction in weekly drinking
    (d = -0.14), stronger in community samples and with paper "volitional help sheets"; effects faded over time; no effect on heavy episodic drinking.

15. **MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine), "Alcohol withdrawal".** https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000764.htm (type: health reference).
    "Alcohol withdrawal is a serious condition that may quickly become life-threatening." Symptoms usually within 8 hours, peak 24 to 72 hours.
    Contact a provider or ER if you might be in withdrawal.
