## Sources

### Books

1. **Women & Psychedelics: Uncovering Invisible Voices** (Spanish: *Mujeres y psicodélicos: descubriendo las voces
   invisibles*). Eds. Erika Dyck, Patrick Farrell, Beatriz Caiuby Labate, Clancy Cavnar, Ibrahim Gabriell, Glauber
   Loures de Assis. Chacruna Institute / Lunaria (Spanish, 2022); Synergetic Press (English, 2024).
   https://chacruna.net/women-psychedelics-invisible-voices/ Type: essay anthology (book).
   Short essays on women's place in psychedelic history, with a Latin American focus. Profiles Wilma Mahua Campos
   (Shipibo ayahuasquera), Madrinha Rita Gregório de Melo, Amada Cardenas (peyotera in south Texas). Calls María
   Sabina "probably the most famous woman associated with psilocybin mushrooms"; her meeting with Wasson had
   "life-changing consequences". Another essay recenters Valentina Wasson.

2. **Women Who Run with the Wolves.** Clarissa Pinkola Estés. Ballantine Books, 1992 (hardcover).
   https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/47111/women-who-run-with-the-wolves-by-clarissa-pinkola-estes-phd
   Spanish ed.: *Mujeres que corren con los lobos*,
   https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/47108/mujeres-que-corren-con-los-lobos--women-who-run-with-the-wolves-by-clarissa-pinkola-estes-phd/9781644735794/
   Type: book (Jungian analyst, myths and stories). Publisher description: "Within every woman there lives a powerful
   force, filled with good instincts, passionate creativity, and ageless knowing." Social pressure "has muffled the
   deep, life-giving messages of our own souls." The book aims to "retrieve" that instinctual nature.

3. **Vida de María Sabina: la sabia de los hongos.** Álvaro Estrada. Siglo XXI Editores, 1977 (current ed. 2022).
   https://www.caniem.org/catalogo-venta-derechos/en/product/vida-de-maria-sabina-la-sabia-de-los-hongos/
   Type: oral autobiography (book). Estrada recorded her life and chants in Mazatec and translated them. She was a
   Mazatec healer who used sacred mushrooms as a link between the earthly and spiritual, and became famous without
   seeking it.

### Talks and interviews

4. **Psilocybin, love and the meaning of life.** Mary Cosimano, TEDxKC, June 2020.
   https://www.ted.com/talks/mary_cosimano_psilocybin_love_and_the_meaning_of_life Type: TEDx talk.
   Cosimano is described by TED as a Johns Hopkins researcher. Talk on guided psilocybin sessions in
   trials; key observation is love and connection: "love is literally part of the answer."

5. **Can magic mushrooms unlock depression?** Rosalind Watts, TEDxOxford, 2018.
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kfGaVAXeMY (transcript read at singjupost.com). Type: TEDx talk.
   Watts, clinical psychologist, clinical lead of Imperial College's psilocybin depression trial. Patients went "from
   disconnection to connection" and "from being emotionally locked up inside to being emotionally liberated". The
   therapist did not direct the content; "the lessons were planned by the most accurate therapist there is:
   themselves." Relief returned for 11 of 20 within about two months (stated in the talk). Credible: led the trial.

6. **How Mushrooms Helped Me Give Up My Dream of Being a Doctor.** Mareesa Stertz, Lucid News / Adventures of the
   Psyche podcast, 15 Nov 2021. https://www.lucid.news/how-mushrooms-helped-me-doctor/ Type: podcast interview,
   first-person account (testimonial) of Dr. Julia Mirer.
   A seemingly uneventful psilocybin experience reconnected her with her intuition and helped her release an
   inherited dream of practicing Western medicine. Pull quote: "I think what I really needed was to connect back to my
   intuition."

### Media and expert writing

8. **Revisiting Nicolás Echevarría's María Sabina, mujer espíritu.** Maya Han, The Brooklyn Rail, November 2025.
   https://brooklynrail.org/2025/11/film/revisiting-nicolas-echevarria-maria-sabina-mujer-espiritu/ Type: film essay.
   Sabina (1894 to 1985), Mazatec sabia; Wasson's Life article helped spark the 1960s counterculture (used in the post). She insisted she
   was "simply the 'interpreter'" through which the niños santos spoke. Chant line: "I am the woman who looks inward."
   The filmed ceremony "foregrounds the females, rendering the event in effect a women's space"; "the sisterhood that
   binds la sabia with her female relatives, friends, and neighbors". Film dated 1978 (other sources say 1979).

9. **María Sabina, Mushrooms, and Colonial Extractivism.** Osiris Sinuhé González Romero, Chacruna, 27 May 2021.
   https://chacruna.net/maria-sabina-mushrooms-and-colonial-extractivism/ Type: expert article.
   Velada: night ceremony. She first met the mushrooms as a child; became Huautla's healer after healing her
   sister. Wasson met her in 1955 under pressure from the municipal trustee. Afterwards her house was burned, her son
   murdered; she died in poverty in 1985.

10. **A Call for Women's Representation in Psychedelic Research.** Alexandra Nigri, Chacruna, 26 Jan 2026.
    https://chacruna.net/?p=130786 Type: expert article by the review's lead author.
    Review of 75 human studies on psychedelics for problematic alcohol or drug use: 9 included no women, 5 did not
    report sex, 11 analyzed sex differences, none discussed sex or gender. "Women were consistently
    underrepresented, rarely analyzed, and almost never discussed."

11. **Ayahuasca Community Guide for the Awareness of Sexual Abuse.** Emily Sinclair and Beatriz Labate, MAPS Bulletin
    vol. 29 no. 1, Spring 2019 (Chacruna Institute). https://maps.org/?p=11043 Type: community guide.
    Sexual abuse and harassment occur in ayahuasca and shamanic healing settings; individual healings are a common
    setting; power imbalance and the medicine's effects raise doubts about consent. Learn the guidelines before
    attending; it is the healer's responsibility not to enter relationships with participants.

### Research

12. **Patients' Accounts of Increased "Connectedness" and "Acceptance" After Psilocybin for Treatment-Resistant
    Depression.** Watts R, Day C, Krzanowski J, Nutt D, Carhart-Harris R. Journal of Humanistic Psychology, 2017,
    57(5):520-564. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022167817709585 Type: qualitative interview study, 20 patients, six months
    after an open-label trial. Shift from disconnection (from self, others, world) to connection, and from emotional
    avoidance to acceptance. Small, no control group, mixed sex.

13. **Patient experiences of psilocybin-assisted psychotherapy: an interpretative phenomenological analysis.** Belser AB
    et al. Journal of Humanistic Psychology, 2017, 57(4):354-388. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022167817706884
    Type: qualitative (IPA), 13 adults with cancer-related anxiety. "Embodiment" found in most transcripts; also
    "surrender or letting go", "a movement from feelings of separateness to interconnectedness", "experiences of
    transient psychological distress".

14. **Psychedelics for substance use disorders: are women being addressed? A scoping review.** Wojdyslawski Nigri A et
    al. International Journal of Drug Policy, 2025, 145:104960. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugpo.2025.104960
    Type: scoping review. Metadata read via Crossref; figures taken from source 10 (lead author's summary).
