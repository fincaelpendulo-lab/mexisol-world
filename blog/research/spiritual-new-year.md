## Sources (all opened and read)

### Books
1. **Michael Norton (2024). The Ritual Effect: From Habit to Ritual, Harness the Surprising Power of Everyday
   Actions.** Scribner. Open Library record https://openlibrary.org/books/OL51646990M (publisher, year). Kirkus review
   (https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/michael-norton/the-ritual-effect/, Jan 2024): habit is "the what",
   ritual is "the how". Author is a Harvard Business School professor.
2. **Pema Chödrön (1997). When Things Fall Apart: Heart Advice for Difficult Times.** Shambhala. Open Library

   Marginalian (2017): "Things falling apart is a kind of testing and also a kind of healing." and "room for grief,
   for relief, for misery, for joy."

   person you want to be." "Prove it to yourself with small wins."

### Media / creators
7. **Carmen Nobel (2013). The Power of Rituals in Life, Death, and Business.** HBS Working Knowledge.
   https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/the-power-of-rituals-in-life-death-and-business. Lab ritual: draw
   feelings, sprinkle salt, tear up, count to ten; woman tore up photos with her ex at the park where they met; most
   rituals personal, not religious. Norton: people who perform rituals report feeling better.
8. **Elizabeth G. Howard. How to Use a Burning Bowl Ritual to Release the Past.** Unity.
   https://unity.org/resources/articles/how-burning-bowl-ritual. Steps: identify, write, burn in a safe place, watch,
   affirm, scatter cooled ashes into wind or water, write intentions. Unity holds one each January as a
   nondenominational alternative to resolutions. "the purpose of the ceremony is to shift consciousness."
9. **Maria Popova (2017). When Things Fall Apart.** The Marginalian.
   https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/07/17/when-things-fall-apart-pema-chodron/ (Chödrön quotes above).
10. **Redacción El Universal (2020). Año Nuevo 2021: los rituales más populares para recibir el año.**
    https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/nacion/sociedad/ano-nuevo-2021-los-rituales-mas-populares-para-recibir-el-ano/

11. **Redacción National Geographic (2024). Año Nuevo: ¿por qué se comen 12 uvas en Nochevieja?**

### Research
15. **Norcross, Mrazek, Fisher, Mrazek (2002). Auld lang syne.** Journal of Clinical Psychology 58(4):397-405.
    https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.1151 (PMID 11920693). 159 resolvers vs 123 nonresolvers; at six months 46% of
    resolvers vs 4% of nonresolvers continuously successful; self-efficacy, skills and readiness predicted success.
16. **Oscarsson, Carlbring, Andersson, Rozental (2020). A large-scale experiment on New Year's resolutions.** PLOS
    ONE 15(12): e0234097. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234097 1,066 Swedish adults; 55% successful at one
    year; approach goals 58.9% vs avoidance 47.1%; self-reported; goal type not randomised.
17. **Norton and Gino (2014). Rituals alleviate grieving for loved ones, lovers, and lotteries.** J Exp Psychol Gen
    143(1):266-272. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031772 Three experiments; rituals reduced grief via regained feelings
    of control, also for people who did not believe in rituals.
18. **Dai, Milkman, Riis (2014). The Fresh Start Effect.** Management Science 60(10):2563-2582.
    https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901 Searches for "diet", gym visits and goal commitments rise after temporal
    landmarks (new year, week, birthday); landmarks put past imperfections in an earlier "period".
