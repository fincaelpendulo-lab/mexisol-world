Mexico is home to one of the oldest living traditions of ceremonial mushroom use. If you are considering a psilocybin retreat, it is a natural place to do it. Here is what to know.

## A long history

Psilocybin mushrooms have been used in ceremony in Mexico for centuries, especially by the Mazatec people in the mountains of Oaxaca, who call them *niños santos*, the holy children. The wider world learned about these night vigils in the 1950s through the Mazatec healer María Sabina.

Today, retreats in Mexico draw on that history while adding medical screening, preparation and integration.

## What psilocybin is like

Many people find mushrooms gentler on the body than ayahuasca. Purging is less common, and the room is lighter: music, open space, room to move or rest. That does not make it easy. Psilocybin can bring up deep emotion, and that is often the point.

Taken by mouth, the effects usually begin after 20 to 40 minutes, peak at one to two hours and last about six hours. More in [psilocybin myths vs. evidence](psilocybin-myths).

## Screening

Psilocybin's main risks are psychological. A personal history of psychosis, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder is a reason not to attend, and some medications need review. Because the week includes a Satori ceremony, the MAO-inhibitor medication list applies too. See our [medical information](../medical#psilocybin).
