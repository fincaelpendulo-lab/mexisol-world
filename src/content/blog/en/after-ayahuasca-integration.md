The ceremony ends. You go back to your room. Now what?

The days after a ceremony matter as much as the night itself. Here is what usually happens in your body and mind, and how to look after both.

## The night of the ceremony

Ceremonies run late, and you go to bed exhausted, often with a mind that is still very awake.

You may fall asleep straight away, or lie awake for hours going over what happened. Both are normal. Don't force sleep, and don't force yourself to stay up. Your body knows what it needs.

## Days one and two

You wake up having been through something.

**Emotionally** you may feel raw and open. Tears and laughter come easily. Or you may feel numb, as if wrapped in a protective bubble. Love, gratitude, grief, fear, awe and confusion can all cycle through in a single day.

**Physically** it is common to feel deeply tired, sensitive to light and sound, a little dehydrated, with a delicate stomach and sore muscles.

**Mentally** it can be hard to focus, while insights and memories keep surfacing.

**What helps:** rest, write, move gently if your body asks for it. Don't try to be productive, and don't try to figure it all out yet. Eat light, drink water and sleep when you can.

## Days three and four

You are slowly coming back. The tiredness starts to lift, emotions settle, sleep and appetite return. Integration is still very active, though, and you may feel tender.

Walk, stretch, journal, draw. Talk with others from the group if you want to, or take quiet time if you need it.

## The first week

You feel more like yourself, but not quite back. Insights keep coming. It can be hard to relate to ordinary conversations at home. Energy may stay lower than usual.

Go back to normal life slowly. Don't take on everything at once.

## The first weeks and months

After about two weeks most people function normally again. Underneath, the work goes on. Many people find that integration takes two to three months: patterns shift, insights turn into decisions, the nervous system settles.

## Food during integration

For the first few days keep eating lightly. Avoid heavy meat, strong spices, sugar, processed food, alcohol and caffeine. Choose fruit, gently cooked vegetables, rice, beans, soups and herbal tea. Bring back your usual food gradually from about day three to five.

Avoid alcohol for at least a week.

## Sleep

Vivid dreams, light sleep and waking several times are common for a few nights. Rest even if you don't sleep. Many people sleep deeply again around day three or four.

## Emotions

Crying, often from release rather than sadness, laughter, joy, fear, anger, tenderness or a protective numbness can all pass through. Feel them, write about them and share them with people who can listen.

If you ever feel unsafe or have thoughts of harming yourself, reach out for help right away, to us and to local emergency services.

## Common worries

**"I feel disconnected from reality."** Your baseline has shifted, and you are seeing things you didn't see before. Give it time.

**"I can't relate to my life anymore."** You had an experience the people around you didn't. Connection returns as you integrate and share with care.

**"I'm doubting everything I experienced."** That is your rational mind catching up. Doubt is part of integration too.

**"I didn't feel much. Was it a waste?"** Some ceremonies are quiet. Insights from quiet nights often arrive slowly.

**"I had a hard night and don't know what to do with it."** Talk about it. At the Finca there is group and one-on-one integration during the week, and we stay in touch after you go home.

## A rough timeline

- **Days 1 to 3:** raw, emotional, tired.
- **Days 4 to 7:** slowly coming back.
- **Week 2:** mostly functional, integration still active.
- **Months 1 to 3:** deeper integration: habits shift and insights become choices.

## When does life feel normal again?

Around the second or third week you will feel mostly yourself. But "normal" has moved. That is not a problem. That is integration.
