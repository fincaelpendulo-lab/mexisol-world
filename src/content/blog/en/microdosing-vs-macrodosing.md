Microdosing vs macrodosing is not a question of more or less of the same thing. A microdose is a small amount taken as part of your routine, so that you barely notice it. A full ceremonial dose is an experience you set a whole day aside for: hours in which your usual sense of yourself loosens and something else has room to speak.

Many women who write to us already microdose at home and wonder what a ceremony would add, and whether they are ready. This guide covers what microdosers report, what the placebo studies found, what changes in a full ceremony, and how we decide the dose with you. We do not give gram amounts, here or anywhere.

## Microdosing vs macrodosing: two ways of meeting the mushroom

The psychologist James Fadiman, who has studied psychedelics since the 1960s and made microdosing widely known, sorts their use by purpose. In *[The Psychedelic Explorer's Guide](https://books.apple.com/us/book/id1249482442)* (2011) he describes "spiritual (high dose), therapeutic (moderate dose), and problem-solving (low dose and microdose)" uses.

That map is useful. A microdose is meant to stay in the background of an ordinary day: work, school runs, a walk. A full dose asks you to stop the day completely, lie down, close your eyes and let the experience lead.

| | Microdose at home | Full ceremony |
|---|---|---|
| What you feel | Little or nothing obvious | A strong, hours-long shift in perception and emotion |
| Where | Your daily life | A held space, set apart from daily life |
| What people seek | A steadier mood, focus, a better day | Insight, meaning, a meeting with what is underneath |
| Who is with you | Usually no one | Guides, musicians and a support team |
| How often | A routine over weeks | A few ceremonies, then time to integrate |

Neither is a "real" version of the other. They are two different relationships with the same medicine.

## What microdosers report, and what the placebo studies found

The book that brought microdosing to many women is Ayelet Waldman's memoir *[A Really Good Day](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/545767/a-really-good-day-by-ayelet-waldman/)* (2017). Her moods had become unmanageable, and she writes, "In my case, it was all because of perimenopause." She kept a diary of a month of microdosing. She used LSD, not mushrooms, and the title comes from what microdosers told Fadiman: "That was a really good day."

People who microdose mushrooms describe similar things. Aubrie Gates, from Colorado, told the [Associated Press in 2025](https://www.kunc.org/news/2025-04-20/believers-say-microdosing-psychedelics-helps-them-scientists-are-trying-to-measure-the-claims) that it "makes you feel viscerally in your body a new way of being, a more healthy way of being."

### The microdosing placebo studies

Researchers have tried to test those reports, and the results are humbling. In a [2021 study in eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.62878), Balázs Szigeti and colleagues at Imperial College London asked 191 people who already microdosed to prepare their own placebo capsules alongside real ones, so nobody knew what they took on a given day. After four weeks, both groups improved, with no significant difference between them. The authors concluded that "anecdotal benefits of microdosing can be explained by the placebo effect."

A [2022 double-blind trial in Translational Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-022-02039-0) with 34 people starting to microdose psilocybin mushrooms found the same pattern. Effects felt stronger on the active dose only among those who correctly guessed which week they were in, and there was no benefit on well-being, creativity or thinking. The authors wrote that "expectation underlies at least some of the anecdotal benefits." Both studies are small and use healthy volunteers, so they settle less than the headlines suggest.

Even Fadiman, at [Psychedelic Science 2025](https://virtualtrip.maps.org/video/microdosing-why-is-it-so-popular-and-how-much-of-what-is-believed-is-true/), framed the question openly: is microdosing a placebo, a panacea, or something in between? Szigeti himself does not dismiss it. On the [Psychedelic Medicine Podcast](https://plantmedicinepodcast.libsyn.com/microdosing-and-the-placebo-effect-with-balzs-szigeti-phd) he called the results "mixed". He later told the AP that the effect is "like a self-fulfilling prophecy," and the neuroscientist Harriet de Wit added that "it may only work in some people and not in other people." The same AP report notes that long-term effects in humans have not been studied.

What this tells us is gentle: intention and expectation are powerful. A microdose may be one way of choosing, every few days, to pay attention to yourself. That matters. It is just not the same as what happens in ceremony.

## What changes in a full ceremony

At a full dose, the medicine is not subtle. Szigeti points out that in high-dose trials "everybody will recognize" when they have had the active substance. You do not wonder whether it is working.

Michael Pollan, who wrote *[How to Change Your Mind](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/529343/how-to-change-your-mind-by-michael-pollan/)* (2018), described his own guided session to [Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/05/30/michael-pollan-on-why-its-a-good-idea-to-lose-your-self/): "I had a fairly high-dose psilocybin trip," and felt "my sense of self kind of crumble." What stayed with him was a discovery: "I wasn't necessarily identical to my ego, which I had always assumed was the case."

That is the shift many women describe when they move from microdosing to macrodosing. The question changes from "how do I feel today?" to "who is the one feeling?" Mood becomes meaning.

### What the research says about meaning

The Johns Hopkins researcher Roland Griffiths spent his later career on exactly this. In a [2006 study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5), 36 adults who had never taken a psychedelic received psilocybin in a calm, supportive room with trained monitors present. In the [14-month follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881108094300), 58% rated it among the five most personally meaningful experiences of their lives, 67% among the five most spiritually significant, and 64% said it had increased their well-being or life satisfaction.

Griffiths told [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/long-trip-magic-mushrooms/) that people compared it to events like the birth of a child. "There's something about the saliency of these experiences that's stunning." In his [TEDMED talk](https://www.tedmed.com/talk/the-science-of-psilocybin-and-its-use-to-relieve-suffering/), he spoke of "spiritually meaningful, personally transformative experiences." It was one small, careful study in a lab, not a promise for anyone.

A full ceremony is also not always pleasant. Old grief can come up, or fear, or a long stretch where nothing makes sense. At MexiSol World we do not speak of good or bad experiences, only experiences. The hard parts are part of the process, which is why being held matters so much. More on this in [psilocybin myths vs. evidence](../blog/psilocybin-myths).

## How dose is decided with us: conversation, not charts

Most pages about microdosing vs macrodosing are dosage charts. We do not publish one, for a reason: the right amount depends on your body, your history, your intention and how you feel that evening, and none of that fits in a table.

On our [psilocybin retreat](../psilocybin-retreat), you sit with Krista before each ceremony and decide your dose together. A few things to know:

- **You can start smaller.** If you come from microdosing and feel unsure, say so. Many guests choose a gentler first ceremony.
- **You can ask for more.** During the ceremony, if you feel ready to go deeper, you can ask for more medicine.
- **Nobody pushes.** Your yes and your no are both respected.

A guest called Duncan, writing about his first ceremonial psilocybin experience with us, [put it on Retreat Guru](https://retreat.guru/centers/1747-1/mexisoul-retreats#reviews) this way: "While clear dosing recommendations were provided, participants were encouraged to choose what felt right for their own comfort level and readiness."

## Set, setting and being held

At home, a microdose sits inside your normal life. In ceremony, the setting is part of the medicine. Griffiths's results came from a supportive room with people present the whole time, and Pollan's advice is plain: "I would urge them to do it with a guide." In his words, "the guide offers a safe place, as they say, to let go."

Our psilocybin week takes place at Finca El Péndulo, near Tepatitlán, Jalisco. It includes two psilocybin ceremonies and one Satori ceremony, for up to 15 guests. The ceremonies have live music and room to move or lie still. Around them are temazcal, Osho meditations, time with the horses, and group and one-on-one integration. Most of our team are women, and our integration coach is a woman.

Integration is where a ceremony becomes daily life. A microdose tries to change your day. A ceremony often shows you what in your life wants to change, and the weeks after are when you do that work. Our [integration guide](../blog/after-ayahuasca-integration) explains how we approach it.

## Are you ready to move from micro to ceremony?

There is no test, but these questions help:

1. **Can you name an intention?** Not a goal to fix yourself, but a question you are willing to sit with.
2. **Are you willing to not be in control for a few hours?** This is the biggest difference from microdosing.
3. **Can you give yourself a full week?** We only offer the full retreat week, with no single-ceremony option, because preparation and integration need time.
4. **Will you be honest in screening?** Tell us that you microdose, what you take and any medication.

Medical screening is required before any retreat. We review your medications, talk about why you are coming and ask for your commitment to the process. Any change to a prescribed medication is made only with the doctor who prescribed it. A personal history of psychosis, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder is a reason not to attend, and because the week includes Satori, the MAO-inhibitor medication list applies too. See our [medical information](../medical). You must arrive sober.

A retreat is not therapy and not a treatment. It is a space for inner work, held with care.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is microdosing just a placebo?**
Two placebo-controlled studies, in 2021 and 2022, found that the benefits people reported were largely explained by expectation. Both were small and used healthy volunteers. Expectation and intention are real forces, but they are not the same as the effects of a full dose.

**Is microdosing spiritual?**
It can be part of a spiritual practice if you use it as a moment of attention. Fadiman links spiritual use with high doses, though, and the meaning that people describe in research comes from full-dose experiences in a held setting.

**I have only microdosed. Will my first macrodose of psilocybin be too much?**
A ceremony is a different experience, so it is normal to feel nervous. With us you decide the dose with Krista, you can start smaller and ask for more, and the team is with you the whole time.

**How much is a full dose of mushrooms in ceremony?**
We do not give gram amounts. Your dose is decided in conversation before each ceremony and adjusted in the moment, based on you, not on a chart.

**Should I choose mushrooms or ayahuasca?**
Mushrooms are often gentler on the body, with less purging. Read [ayahuasca or psilocybin](../blog/ayahuasca-or-psilocybin) to compare.
