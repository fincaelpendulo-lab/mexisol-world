Peru offers the deepest living tradition and the rainforest itself. Mexico offers a much shorter trip, an easier climate and quicker access to a road and a city. Choosing between ayahuasca Mexico vs Peru comes down to what you value most for this ceremony: the source of the tradition, or time, cost and care close to home.

We run ayahuasca retreats at Finca El Péndulo in Jalisco, so we are not neutral. We will say plainly where Peru does better. If you have sat in the Amazon before, or you are weighing your first trip, this is the comparison we would want to read.

## The short answer

Choose Peru if being in the Amazon, with the communities this medicine comes from, is the point for you, and you have the time and budget for a longer trip.

Choose Mexico if you want a held week with fewer travel days, lower total cost and a gentler setting for the body, and you are fine with the medicine coming to you rather than going to it.

| | Peru (Amazon) | Mexico (Jalisco highlands) |
|---|---|---|
| Tradition | The source: native Amazonian communities, declared national heritage | Imported medicine, alongside Mexico's own traditions |
| Getting there | International flight, domestic flight to Iquitos or Pucallpa, often a boat | One flight to Guadalajara, about an hour by road |
| Setting | Rainforest | Highlands, warm days, cool nights |
| Health prep | CDC lists malaria and yellow fever for the Iquitos area | Jalisco not listed for either |
| Medical access | Centres often remote | On a road, near a town |

## Tradition and lineage: what Peru offers

This is where Peru wins, and it is not close. In 2008, Peru's National Institute of Culture declared the traditional knowledge and uses of ayahuasca by native Amazonian communities part of the nation's cultural heritage. The [resolution, reported by Peru's state news agency Andina](https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-declaran-patrimonio-cultural-de-nacion-a-los-conocimientos-y-usos-tradicionales-del-ayahuasca-184254.aspx), aims to protect the sacred character of the ritual, as distinct from commercial use.

In Shipibo communities near Pucallpa, healers often come from families of healers and train for years with the plants. A [Cultural Survival report](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/publications/cultural-survival-quarterly/tourism-opens-new-doors-creates-new-challenges-traditional) describes that training and also the pressure tourism puts on it. To drink in the place where the songs and the plant knowledge were born is something no other country can give you.

Ayahuasca tourism there is also large and long established. [AFP reported in 2025](https://www.swissinfo.ch/spa/la-fiscal%C3%ADa-investiga-la-muerte-de-un-estadounidense-tras-consumir-ayahuasca-en-la-amazon%C3%ADa-peruana/89474970) that spiritual tourism around ayahuasca has grown for more than two decades in Loreto, Ucayali and San Martín. That means many choices, and a wide range of quality.

### Where our lineage comes from

We will not pretend Jalisco is the Amazon. Our ayahuasca is a brew of the vine and the chacruna leaf, prepared in Pucallpa, Peru. Humberto, who leads our ceremonies, was born in Tepatitlán, a few minutes from the Finca, and trained in Peru through long master plant dietas.

So the medicine and the training come from Peru. What Mexico adds are its own traditions around the week: temazcal, cacao and fire. If lineage questions matter to you, ask any centre, in either country, who trained the facilitator, for how long and with whom.

## Getting there: flights, connections and days off

Iquitos, the main hub for ayahuasca tourism in Peru, is a city of around 430,000 people that is [accessible only by air and river](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/101153-largest-city-with-no-major-road-connection). From Lima, the domestic flight takes about [1 hour 40 minutes, according to Peru's official tourism site](https://www.peru.travel/en/destinations/loreto). Some centres sit far outside the city: the lodge in the AFP report was about five hours by river from Iquitos.

Guadalajara has direct flights from many cities in Mexico, the United States and Canada. The Finca is about an hour from the airport by toll road. If your flight lands on arrival day with time to spare, you can be here for dinner. Details are in [how to get to Finca El Péndulo](../blog/getting-to-finca-el-pendulo).

### Count the days, not just the retreat

A seven-day retreat is never seven days away. For Peru, add the international flight, the domestic flight, any night in Lima or Iquitos your connections require, and the river or road transfer. For Jalisco, a flight that lands on arrival day can get you to the Finca before dinner, and the last day ends with the ride to the airport. The first ceremony is on day two.

Less travel also means arriving more rested for that first night.

## The full cost of an ayahuasca retreat in Mexico vs Peru

Retreat prices vary enormously in both countries, and a listed price rarely tells you the full cost. Compare the trip, not the program. Here is what to add up for either country:

1. **The retreat itself.** What is included: ceremonies, meals, room, integration, transfers?
2. **International flights.** To Lima, or to Guadalajara.
3. **Domestic legs.** A flight to Iquitos or Pucallpa, a boat, or a road transfer.
4. **Extra nights.** Hotels before or after, if connections do not line up.
5. **Travel health.** For the Amazon, a travel clinic visit and any vaccine or preventive medicine you are prescribed.
6. **Time off work.** Days lost to travel are a cost too.

## Climate, setting and physical demands

Ayahuasca is demanding on the body wherever you drink it. In the large [Global Ayahuasca Survey](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0000438) (Bouso and colleagues, 2022, an online survey of 10,836 people), 69.9% reported at least one acute physical effect and 62% reported vomiting or nausea. Physical health conditions were linked to more adverse physical effects.

So the setting around the ceremony matters. The Amazon gives you the rainforest at night, which many people find powerful. It also brings health preparation: the [CDC recommends malaria prevention for the Iquitos area](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/peru) and the yellow fever vaccine for lower parts of Loreto.

In Mexico, the [CDC does not list Jalisco as a malaria area](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/mexico) and does not recommend the yellow fever vaccine. The highlands are warm by day and cool at night for most of the year. Between ceremonies there are real beds, a pool, a lake and food cooked on site.

One practical note: any vaccine or medicine a travel clinic gives you is part of your medication list. Every medication has to be reviewed before ayahuasca, which contains an MAO inhibitor.

## Safety: screening and access to medical care

Good and careless centres exist in both countries. Geography changes how easy it is to get help when something goes wrong.

The [UK government's travel advice for Peru](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/peru/safety-and-security) says the brew offered to tourists is not regulated, that people have become seriously ill and some have died, and that retreats are usually far from populated areas, "making it difficult to get medical attention". It also mentions reports of sexual assault. An anthropologist writing for [Chacruna in 2018](https://chacruna.net/should-ayahuasca-tourism-peru-be-regulated/) counted around 150 centres and suppliers in the Iquitos region, most of them unregistered.

Disclosure matters everywhere. In a 2025 death in Loreto, [El Comercio reported](https://elcomercio.pe/peru/loreto/loreto-norteamericano-muere-tras-sesion-de-ayahuasca-ultimas-noticia/) that the guest had not mentioned he was taking antibiotics. In a [2015 study of 77 travellers](https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2015.1094590), the risks people named included lack of trust in the shaman or organizer and inaccurate information from them.

It has three parts: a review of every medication you take and of any taper, which is always planned with the doctor who prescribed it; a conversation about why you are coming; and your commitment to the process. Read your own government's current advice for whichever region you choose. ## Who tends to choose each

People go to the Amazon for many reasons. In a classic [interview study of ayahuasca seekers in Amazonia](https://doi.org/10.1080/02791072.2005.10399803), the main motives were spiritual development, emotional healing and self-awareness, not thrill seeking. Those reasons can be honored in either country.

**Peru tends to suit you if:**

- you want to sit with the medicine in its home, with Amazonian healers
- you have two weeks or more, and a long trip feels like part of the ceremony
- you are comfortable with heat, insects and remote places
- you are planning a long dieta or a stay of several weeks

**Mexico tends to suit you if:**

- you have one week, not two
- you want fewer flights and lower total cost
- you want ceremony held inside a full week, with integration, temazcal and rest
- you want medical access to be a drive away

Anthropologist Evgenia Fotiou, who did her doctoral fieldwork in Iquitos, notes in a [2024 University of Exeter keynote](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Px88jbsMd4) that ayahuasca retreats now run "in Peru and beyond", and that how the ceremony is framed helps people integrate what happens. That is true anywhere: the people and the care count more than the map.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Mexico or Peru better for ayahuasca?**
Neither is better for everyone. Peru offers the source tradition and the rainforest; Mexico offers less travel, lower total cost and easier access to care. The quality of the centre and the facilitator matters more than the country.

**Is ayahuasca in Mexico the same as in Peru?**
The brew can be the same. Ours is made from the ayahuasca vine and chacruna leaf, prepared in Pucallpa, Peru. The setting, the songs and the program around the ceremonies differ from centre to centre.

**What is the shortest flight to an ayahuasca retreat from the US?**
From much of the US, a direct flight to Guadalajara is far shorter than reaching Iquitos via Lima and a domestic flight. Check routes from your own city, and count transfers at both ends.

**Do I need vaccines for an ayahuasca retreat in Peru?**
The CDC recommends malaria prevention for the Iquitos area and the yellow fever vaccine for lower parts of Loreto. See a travel clinic, and tell your retreat about anything they prescribe, since every medicine must be reviewed before ayahuasca.

**Is ayahuasca legal in Mexico or Peru?**
We do not give legal advice. Peru has recognized the traditional uses of ayahuasca as cultural heritage since 2008. Laws differ by country and change, so check the rules that apply to you.
