Ayahuasca ego death is the experience of your usual sense of "I" falling away during a ceremony. Some people feel they are dying, or that they have already died. It is common among experienced drinkers, it is often described as one of the most meaningful nights of their lives, and it is not the goal of the ceremony.

This article is for people who have sat with the medicine before, or have read a great deal, and want to understand what actually dissolves, what research has found, and what to do with it once you are back in your own life.

## What people mean by ayahuasca ego death

"Ego" here does not mean arrogance. It means the part of you that says "this is me, that is the world." It keeps your story, defends it and tells you who you are from moment to moment.

A research team that included Robin Carhart-Harris and David Nutt calls this experience ego dissolution. In [a 2016 paper in Frontiers in Human Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00269) they define it as "a compromised sense of 'self'" and call it a key feature of the psychedelic experience. The scale they built asks people whether they felt "a decrease in my sense of self-importance" or "far less absorbed by my own issues and concerns."

Contemplatives got there long before the scientists. In [The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/187467/the-book-by-alan-watts/) (1966), Alan Watts argued that the feeling of being a separate self, cut off from the rest of the universe, is a kind of illusion. Ayahuasca ego death is the night that illusion stops working for a while.

## What an ego death experience feels like, in people's own words

People rarely describe it as peaceful at first. Often it feels like dying.

The journalist Bekah Congdon wrote about her ayahuasca retreat in Peru for [Reason](https://reason.com/2025/07/28/cusco-peru/). During her second ceremony, she writes, "I suddenly became convinced that I had died." What came after surprised her: "I felt a shift. The emotional storms had passed. I was reborn."

Michael Pollan describes his own ego dissolution, with psilocybin rather than ayahuasca, in [How to Change Your Mind](https://michaelpollan.com/books/how-to-change-your-mind/). Talking it through on [The Tim Ferriss Show](https://tim.blog/2019/03/21/michael-pollan-sxsw/), he named the lesson he kept: "I'm not identical to my ego, that I can let my ego go and not be obliterated."

One of our own guests, Jannie B., wrote in her [public review on BookRetreats](https://bookretreats.com/r/7-day-ayahuasca-retreat-at-an-authentic-finca-in-mexico-69537): "My heart was cracked open and I was finally able to reconnect with my true self and inner peace."

Notice what these accounts share. Something dies, and someone is still there afterwards to say so. What dissolves is a story about who you are, not you.

## What a 2023 study of experienced drinkers found

In 2023 a team from Israel and Spain published [two studies in Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1287961) on what they call ayahuasca-induced personal death experiences. They describe it as "an overwhelmingly authentic and convincing sensation of acutely dying or being dead."

| | Study 1 | Study 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Who took part | 54 veteran ayahuasca users | 305 ayahuasca users, online survey in Israel |
| Reported a personal death experience | 36 people (66.7%) | 150 people (49.2%) |
| What went with it | A stronger sense of transcending death | More certainty that consciousness continues after death, more life fulfilment, more problem-focused coping |

Some findings are as important as the headline numbers:

- **It was not a sign of fragility.** The experience was not linked to age, personality, depression, anxiety or depersonalization.
- **It did not lower fear of death.** The authors expected less death anxiety and did not find it. What changed was how people related to death, not whether they feared it.
- **It is a snapshot, not proof.** Both studies are cross-sectional and self-reported, and most participants were in Israel. They cannot show that the experience caused any change. [PsyPost's summary](https://www.psypost.org/new-study-reveals-how-common-ayahuasca-induced-death-experiences-are-and-their-link-to-personal-transformation/) makes the same point.

So for a seasoned drinker, a death-like night is common, it is usually felt as powerful, and it tends to sit alongside a broader view of life and death. That is what the data can say. It cannot promise you anything.

## Is ego death the goal? Ego dissolution versus ego inflation

No. We do not set ego death, or any particular experience, as the aim of a ceremony. People who chase it often find the chasing is itself the ego at work.

The psychologist Clancy Cavnar, who has worked with ayahuasca for years, describes the brew in [an essay for Chacruna](https://chacruna.net/the-effects-of-ayahuasca-on-the-ego/) as able to both contract and expand the ego. Contraction is the humbling night of remorse and honesty. Expansion can bring compassion and unity, and it can also feed self-importance. In her words: "The hungry ego, once fed, becomes ravenous and marvels at itself."

That second risk has a name: ego inflation, or spiritual ego. You come home sure you have been chosen, that you see what others cannot, that the medicine confirmed your greatness. The psychologist David Londoño, who coordinates the ICEERS Support Center, told [ICEERS](https://www.iceers.org/plant-medicine-ego-reintegration/) that he often sees this kind of inflation, and drew a clear line: "There is a difference between transcending and abandoning or losing the ego."

Interestingly, the 2016 ego dissolution survey also measured ego inflation, unusually high self-assurance, and found it linked to cocaine, not to psychedelics. The medicine itself tends to shrink the self. What we do with the memory afterwards is where the ego can sneak back in, wearing robes.

| Ego dissolution | Ego inflation |
|---|---|
| "I am less important than I thought" | "I am more important than others know" |
| Feels like loss, then relief | Feels like a reward |
| Leaves you softer with people | Leaves you superior to people |
| Makes you curious about your own blind spots | Makes you sure you no longer have any |

## Ego, surrender and the contemplative traditions

The traditions have always treated the ego as something to see through, not something to kill.

In a 1976 talk, [Motives for Spiritual Practice](https://beherenownetwork.com/ram-dass-here-and-now-ep-147-motives-for-spiritual-practice/), Ram Dass warned that our motives for practice can free us or keep us stuck in our own egos. We can take sacred things, he said, and "convert them to things around our ego." In the same talk he made a point that matters for anyone with a busy inner life: first you become somebody, then you can begin the journey toward becoming nobody.

Two years earlier, at Naropa, Ram Dass sat on [a panel on the notion of ego](https://beherenownetwork.com/ram-dass-now-ep-112-notion-of-ego/) with the Tibetan teacher Chögyam Trungpa. Trungpa put it gently: "There is another version of survival, other than trying to survive with the basic necessity of the ego."

The philosopher Chris Letheby makes a similar case from a different direction. In [Philosophy of Psychedelics](https://doi.org/10.1093/med/9780198843122.001.0001) (Oxford University Press, 2021), he argues that psychedelics lead to lasting benefits "by altering the sense of self." The point is a looser grip on the self, not its destruction.

Pollan also gave the ego its due in the Ferriss interview: "there are very good reasons to have an ego." It wrote his book. It also, in his words, can become "a tyrant."

### How to hold it in ceremony

Surrender is the practical side of all this. If you feel yourself dying, you do not need to fight it or to make it happen. You can let it be what it is.

- **Breathe and let go of the steering wheel.** The fear is part of the experience, not a sign that something is going wrong.
- **Ask for help if you need it.** Congdon wrote that she found "the strength, not weakness, that it takes to be vulnerable and admit I couldn't do it alone." In ceremony with us, Humberto and the servers are there all night for exactly that.
- **Do not grade the night.** At MexiSol World we say there are no good or bad experiences, only experiences. A night where nothing dissolves can teach as much as a night where everything does.

## Coming back: integrating a dissolved self

The ego returns. Pollan says his came back "in uniform, back on patrol" within days. That is normal, and it is fine. The work is to come back with a self that holds itself more lightly.

Londoño's advice to ICEERS is simple: "It is not about breaking or fighting with the ego, but rather building a healthy ego." Here is what that can look like in practice:

1. **Write the night down without decorating it.** Note what you let go of, not just what you saw.
2. **Watch for the special feeling.** If you notice yourself telling the story to impress, pause. That is the ego reaching for the experience.
3. **Choose one change in how you treat people.** Ego dissolution that matters shows up as patience, listening and fewer defences.
4. **Keep a daily practice.** Pollan noted that experienced meditators show the same quieting of the brain's self-network. A short sit keeps the door open.
5. **Talk to someone who can reflect you honestly.** A teacher, a therapist or an integration circle can tell you when insight is turning into inflation.

### A note on safety

Not everyone should seek the loosening of the self. Cavnar warns that people with a fragile ego structure, such as those with a history of psychosis, may find ayahuasca further weakens their sense of self. Schizophrenia, psychosis, bipolar disorder and dissociative disorders are on our list of conditions that may make ayahuasca unsafe; see our [medical information page](../medical).

Medical screening is required before every retreat. Any change to a prescribed medication is made only with the doctor who prescribed it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is ego death on ayahuasca?**
It is the experience of your ordinary sense of self dissolving during a ceremony, sometimes felt as dying or being dead. Researchers call it ego dissolution or, for ayahuasca, a personal death experience. It usually passes within the ceremony, and people often describe it as deeply meaningful.

**Is ego death dangerous?**
For most screened people it is a temporary experience, and in the 2023 study it was not linked to depression, anxiety or depersonalization. It can be frightening, which is why a held space and experienced facilitators matter. People with a history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face real risks, and medical screening is required.

**How do I know if I had an ego death?**
People often report losing the sense of being a separate person, feeling one with everything, or being convinced they had died. If you are unsure, you probably had a softer version of it, which is just as valid. What matters more is what you do with the night afterwards.

**What is the difference between ego death and ego inflation?**
Ego dissolution leaves you feeling less important and more connected. Ego inflation leaves you feeling special or chosen. The second often appears after a powerful ceremony, and honest integration is how you catch it.

**Can I prepare for ego death?**
You cannot plan it, and we do not promise it. You can prepare for surrender: a clear intention, a calm body, a daily practice before the retreat, and trust in the people holding the space.
